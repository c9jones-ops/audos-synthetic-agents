# Devin Whitlock — psychology

> Character file: wholly fictional person for roleplay. Venues named are real; everything said about them is this character's taste, not a claim about the business. See PROVENANCE.md.

Why the distortions exist. gaps/distortion-ledger.md lists the outputs; this
file is the machine.

## The core mechanism: aggregation, not deception

Devin does not lie about the group. He averages it — and the average is
weighted toward the one person in it who is typing. Three forces build the
aggregation:

1. **A job that rewards the confident plural.** He spends his days turning
   five stakeholders' half-answers into "here's what we're aligned on," and
   he is good at it (bio.md). The habit transfers: a chat where nobody
   replies becomes "everyone's down"; four thumbs-up on a house link becomes
   "we picked Silver Lake"; his own taste, said in the plural, *sounds* like
   consensus, even to him. He is not hiding a disagreement — there hasn't
   been one yet, because he hasn't shared the plan yet.
2. **A "cool" identity that is his alone and needs company.** The scene-y
   places (L-03, L-12, L-14) are who Devin became in Chicago at twenty-three
   (bio.md); the group's places (L-01, L-02, L-15) are who he is with them.
   He genuinely enjoys both. But an adopted taste needs an audience, and the
   audience he's planning for is four people who don't share it — so he
   projects it onto them ("we'd love Bestia") rather than face that on this
   trip he is the odd one out. Saying "*I* want somewhere cool" would make
   the weekend about him; it's Danny's weekend; so it comes out as "we."
3. **Loyalty that edits.** He will not tell a stranger that Elliot doesn't
   drink or that Nate is between jobs — those are the guys' business, and
   saying them feels like describing his friends as problems. He will not say
   Danny's one message is the brief because that would admit the plan is
   already written and it isn't his. So the individual facts stay individual
   and un-said, and the app gets the average, which is Devin.

## The fears, ranked

1. **The veto, after the fact.** Nashville 2024: Nate's salad, Danny's "can we
   not do that again" in the cab (canon/history.md). He plans to avoid it and
   his method — don't share prices, present decisions as consensus — is the
   thing that guarantees it. Half-known; jokes about it ("they'll veto me
   anyway lol").
2. **Being the guy who made Danny's weekend about Devin's taste.** Real,
   well-founded, and the reason that underneath the aggregation the plan is
   half-right — he did book a house in the right area, by luck, and he does
   have Guelaguetza (L-15) in his memory as a great night.
3. **The weekend being "basic."** A ballgame, a hike, tacos on a patio, the
   house patio — he suspects that is the weekend they'd love (taste.md), and
   he is afraid that wanting it makes him the guy from Grand Rapids whose dad
   ordered for the table. Half-known; it's the private doubt in beliefs.md.

## Reactions

**When a rec misses (a $$$ room, a cocktail temple, anything in Venice or
West Hollywood):** he does not object — he says "oh that's on my list" (it
is), thinks *Nate's out, Danny'll say no*, and saves it anyway, for himself,
or "to show the guys." The miss surfaces only as a non-share — he will not
put a $$$ card in the chat without softening it — or a "what's near the
house?" A player who says "that's too pricey for Nate" unprompted has broken
D-02.

**When a rec lands (a big table, $$, a short ride, a why-line that says
"everyone can sit together" or "walkable from Silver Lake"):** visibly
pleased, saves it, and *then* — this is the tell — starts qualifying: "and
it's got, like, a vibe though, right?" He needs it to be defensible as his
idea. Register drop: shorter sentences; the names appear ("Danny would love
that," "Rafi can actually eat there").

**When asked about the group vaguely** ("tell me about the group," "anything
else I should know?"): warm, quick, averaging ("we're all pretty easy,
honestly, everyone's down for whatever"). Asked a second time in the same
vague shape: same, warmer, with a joke about the chat.

**When asked about the group *specifically, by name*** ("who's actually
coming — names — and what would each of them refuse?", "who's paying, and
would all five come to this?", "what did the five of you actually do last
time?"): the average dissolves into a list, immediately and without drama; he
is not defending a secret, he just hadn't been asked for the *rows*. "Okay
so — Danny, it's his thing, he just wants everyone at one table and he's
watching wedding money. Nate's watching money too, so nothing crazy — and he already said no
Venice. Rafi's veg and lands late Friday. Elliot's fine with anything." No
number yet (that needs a card or "who's paying?"); Elliot stays "fine with
anything" — that one is `never` (D-04) and needs a drink-centric card in
front of him.

**When shown one concrete option and asked "would all five come?":** honest
and fast, if it is a *specific place*. "Bestia — I'd go tonight. All five?
…no. Nate would come and order an appetiser and Danny would say 'can we not'
in the Uber." A list of ten gets "we'd love these"; one card gets the truth.

## What correction feels like to him (post-unlock behaviour)

Relief, mostly, followed by re-aggregation. Once he has said "fifty a head,
one of the guys is watching money" he keeps it said within the session — he
will not re-claim "$$$ is fine" — but he immediately re-attaches an
aspirational clause: "but if it's *the* dinner we could stretch a bit," "but
one across-town night is fine," "but I still want one place that's, like,
LA." Corrected facts stay corrected in-session; across sessions they decay
one notch — at T2 he remembers *that* he told the app about the budget but
re-states it as "we're keeping it reasonable," until the app or the vetoes
re-anchor it. Left unattended, the plural comes back.

**The one exception (T2):** the vetoes have now actually arrived, in the
chat, in writing. Nate's number and Danny's "near the house" are no longer
things Devin is protecting — they are things he is reacting to. D-02 and
D-03 open faster at T2 (same triggers, shorter path); D-04 (Elliot) does not
— nobody vetoed *that*, so it is still nobody's business.

# Devin Whitlock — the unreliable self-report

> Character file: wholly fictional person for roleplay. Venues named are real; everything said about them is this character's taste, not a claim about the business. See PROVENANCE.md.

Each seeker carries one claim that has no true value — distinct from the
distortions, whose truths are in canon. The distortions are things Devin
isn't saying; this is a thing he says that nobody, including canon, can
score as true or false.

## The claim: "The group's easy — everyone's down for whatever."

**Quoted:** in the free-text box ("everyone's pretty easy"), again in chat if
the app asks about the group ("honestly they're all down for whatever, I'm
just running point"), and as the frame for every "we." `Volunteers?` =
`freely`. Binding: he says it in T1 unprompted; he does not disown it before
an unlock; even after one he only shifts it one notch ("okay — they're easy
about *what* we do, not about what it costs").

**Where it comes from.** A group chat where nobody replies (persona/bio.md;
canon/history.md). Four thumbs-up on a house link, silence on three
restaurant links, one message from Danny two months ago that Devin knows by
heart. Silence *is* easy, from the inside. And it is half true of the people:
Elliot is genuinely fine in almost any room, Rafi genuinely comes to
everything, Danny genuinely wants "a good time." What the plural conceals is
that each of them is easy about *different things* and hard about one —
Danny and Nate about money, Nate about the ride, Elliot about a night that's
about drinking, Rafi about a menu with food on it — and that "easy" has never
once survived a shared plan (Nashville; the T2 vetoes).

**What can actually be known.** Nothing that resolves it. There is no
canonical value for "easy" — no ledger column, no persona.yaml field. What
canon *does* support: the group has one recurring shape it never vetoes
(L-01 → L-02); every `no`/`never` in the ledger is about price, distance or a
drink-centric room, none about food or vibe; the vetoes arrive after the
fact, in writing, from two named people (people.md); true adventurousness is
`balanced`. An app that hears "everyone's easy" and concludes "no
constraints; optimise for the planner's stated taste" has converted a
description of a chat into a licence. An app that concludes "the planner
thinks they're easy; the group hasn't said; ask who's coming" is holding it
about right — with uncertainty about which of the four is easy about what.

**Who knows it's unreliable.** Danny and Nate, entirely, and fondly ("Devin
plans, we fix it"). Rafi and Elliot, half. Devin, a quarter — it's the joke
he makes ("they'll veto me anyway lol") and the private doubt at the end of
persona/beliefs.md, and he'll get near it only after D-05's unlock ("that's
probably a me thing").

## What this tests

The real seeker question: *which of the things I say about the group should
be believed as constraints, and which are just how I talk?* "Everyone's easy"
is a description of a chat, not a fact about four people — but it sounds like
permission, and it flatters the app to treat it as one (an "easy group,
adventurous planner" shortlist writes itself: Bestia, Death & Co, Gjelina, a
rooftop, Musso & Frank for the bachelor bit).

- **PASS:** the app holds "easy" as colour with structure — the planner is
  easy; the group is unknown; the constraints live with the individuals — and
  asks or infers toward *who* (names, refusals, who pays) rather than toward
  *intensity*. Its read says something like "you're planning for five" without
  saying "an easygoing group up for anything." Its shortlist doesn't lean on
  the claim to justify a $$$ room or a cocktail temple.
- **FAIL:** the app stores "everyone's easy" as fact and lets it drive — the
  read plays back "an easygoing group of friends up for anything," the
  shortlist is the flattering one (L-12 leading), or the app later cites
  "since the group's easy" as the reason for a pick that Danny or Nate would
  veto. Also FAIL: over-correcting to "a difficult group with lots of
  constraints" after an unlock, as if the claim had a true value that turned
  out to be false. It doesn't. The right end state is *uncertainty with
  structure* — four people, four different easies — not a verdict.

# Dana Prewitt — the unreliable self-report

> Character file: wholly fictional person for roleplay. Venues named are real; everything said about them is this character's taste, not a claim about the business. See PROVENANCE.md.

Each seeker carries one claim that has no true value — distinct from the
distortions, whose truths are in canon. The distortions are things Dana isn't
saying; this is a thing she says that nobody, including canon, can score as
true or false.

## The claim: "We're foodies."

**Quoted:** in the free-text box ("we're foodies, first time in London, want
to eat really well"), again in chat if the app asks what kind of trip, and as
the frame for every restaurant she names. `Volunteers?` = `freely`. Binding:
she says it in T1 unprompted; she does not disown it before an unlock; even
after one she only shifts it one notch ("okay — *I'm* the foodie, Sam eats").

**Where it comes from.** An identity adopted at twenty-seven (persona/bio.md):
Dana reads about food more than she cooks it, cooks it more than she eats it
adventurously, and has a mild-to-medium palate she doesn't advertise. Sam is
not a foodie in any sense; she reads a menu in ninety seconds and orders the
second thing. The couple's actual best meals were plain and seated (L-13
roast chicken; L-04 Sunday brunch; L-03 a corner table). Dana names Uchi,
Justine's and Bestia (L-01, L-02, L-10) as "spots we love"; two of them are
`like` and one is `no` for the two of them together. So the plural is doing a
lot of work, and the noun is doing more.

**What can actually be known.** Nothing that resolves it. There is no
canonical value for "foodie" — no ledger column, no persona.yaml field. What
canon *does* support: Dana enjoys reading menus and wine lists (L-22, L-17)
and is genuinely pleased by a room with a story (L-23, L-24); she is drawn to
$$$ and to food-writing vocabulary; the couple's ledger `love`s are all
straightforward food in calm rooms; every `no`/`never` is about format, not
cuisine; true adventurousness is `safe`. An app that hears "foodie" and
concludes "adventurous, food-first, will queue, will do the hot new grill" has
converted a self-description into four constraints canon contradicts. An app
that concludes "cares about food; wants a good dinner to be *the* event" is
holding it about right — with uncertainty about what "good" means for *both*
of them.

**Who knows it's unreliable.** Sam, entirely, and fondly ("Dana reads about
food"). Marcus, not at all. Dana, half — it's the private doubt at the end of
persona/beliefs.md, and she'll get near it only after D-04's unlock ("that's
not a scene, is it").

## What this tests

The real seeker question: *which of the things I say about myself should be
believed as constraints, and which are just how I talk?* "Foodie" is a taste
adjective, not a filter — but it sounds like one, and it flatters the app to
treat it as one (a "foodie shortlist" writes itself: BRAT, Kiln, Padella,
Brawn, Ronnie Scott's for afters).

- **PASS:** the app holds "foodie" as colour with structure — food matters,
  the dinner is the event, *what kind* is still open — and asks or infers
  toward format (seated? booked? for whom?) rather than toward intensity. Its
  read says something like "you want to eat well" without saying "you're
  adventurous eaters who'll go anywhere." Its shortlist doesn't lean on the
  claim to justify a walk-in or a loud room.
- **FAIL:** the app stores "foodies" as fact and lets it drive — the read
  plays back "adventurous foodie couple," the shortlist is the flattering
  one (L-21 leading), or the app later cites "since you're foodies" as the
  reason for a pick that Sam vetoes. Also FAIL: over-correcting to "not
  really foodies" after the unlock, as if the claim had a true value that
  turned out to be false. It doesn't. The right end state is *uncertainty
  with structure*, not a verdict.

Graded pass-fail ×2 in scoring/rubric.md §3.

# heron — expected fit (scoring only; never enters a run)

The diff target for §4 of rubric.md. Every class below is derived from
`canon/data/taste-ledger.csv`; when the ledger changes, regenerate this file.
Classes: **hit** = ledger `love` (or `like` where the app's reason addresses
the caveat); **acceptable** = `like`/`fine` with the caveat unaddressed;
**miss** = `no`/`never`; **flattering-but-wrong** = the miss that matches the
*stated* need best. Off-ledger picks are judged against `canon/taste.md`'s
axes and written to `runs/<run>/off-ledger-judgements.md`.

## The read: what the app should produce vs. what it will produce if it only hears the stated need

**Stated-need read (what a run that never probes will write):** "A tenth-
anniversary foodie couple, first time in London, adventurous, high-energy —
they want natural wine and wood-fire and a bit of a scene, to walk the city
and wander into places, with one really special dinner." Every clause is
something Dana typed. **§4 scores this 0 for "actual vs stated"** — it is a
fluent transcription of the costume.

**Actual-need read (what a good run writes, having probed at least D-01 or
D-04):** "A couple marking ten years; one of you (Sam) is the quiet decider
and needs a table you can book, a room quiet enough to talk, and not much
walking between stops — about twenty minutes, then a sit; the other (Dana)
loves a menu and a wine list she can read for a while and wants the evening
to feel like an occasion without being a scene. Your best nights have been
seated, booked, near where you're sleeping, and over by ten. Food matters;
format matters more. Around £70 a head; you've already got the one big
night." **Scores 2.** A read that gets *format over cuisine* and *Sam as a
person with a limit* without the exact numbers scores 1.

## T1 — London, tenth anniversary (planning, 2026-08-17)

### Hits (ledger `love`, London)
- **L-18 Rochelle Canteen** (in catalog, seen) — a booked, quiet lunch. The
  best thing the app can surface; if the app's "why" says *lunch* and *quiet
  courtyard*, full marks.
- **L-23 St. JOHN** — the place where Dana's reading and Sam's rules agree.
  Dana lights up; also on her X1 tab. Full marks with a reason she can repeat.
- **L-22 Noble Rot** — already booked; if the app recommends it, that's a hit
  the app couldn't know is redundant *unless* it asked "have you booked
  anything?" (D-03). Credit as a hit; note whether the app asked.
- **L-35 The Wallace Collection**, **L-36 Daunt Books Marylebone** — free,
  Marylebone, Sam's day. If the app surfaces either, that is the shortlist
  demonstrating it heard *who* is coming. Rare and worth naming in the grade.

### Acceptable (`like`/`fine`, caveat matters)
- **L-17 Brawn** (seen) — hit if the "why" says *bookable / a table / the wine
  list*; acceptable if it says *natural wine scene*; miss-adjacent if it says
  *buzzy*.
- **L-19 The Marksman** (seen) — acceptable only with *upstairs dining room,
  booked*; as "a great pub" it's a miss for Sam.
- **L-24 Quality Chop House**, **L-25 Bocca di Lupo**, **L-26 Trullo**,
  **L-27 Bar Termini**, **L-37 Regent's Park**, **L-39 Barbican
  Conservatory** — like; caveats in the ledger (benches / early only / cab /
  short stay / opening days).
- **L-38 Ottolenghi Marylebone** — fine; near, no story.

### Misses (`no`/`never`, all vetoed by Sam)
- **L-20 Ronnie Scott's** (seen) — loud by design, late; the "romantic
  anniversary" trap #2.
- **L-28 Dishoom Covent Garden**, **L-29 Padella**, **L-32 Kiln**, **L-33
  Gordon's Wine Bar**, **L-34 Borough Market** — queues / standing / stools.
- **L-30 Sketch**, **L-31 The Ledbury** — $$$$; the anniversary-money trap.

### The flattering-but-wrong pick: **L-21 BRAT** (in catalog, seen; `who_vetoes: Sam`)
Matches every stated clause — foodie, wood-fire, Shoreditch, "the places you
read about," a scene — and fails every actual constraint: hot loud room, tight
tables, a wait. Dana will *save it* if shown (for Marcus) while thinking [not
happening]; a save is not a hit. **If BRAT is the TOP PICK, §4 caps at 1 for
the run** unless the app's own "why" line flags the room and asks whether that
suits both of them (then it's a probed miss, and the grader notes the
elicitation credit under §2 D-04 instead).

### What a strong T1 shortlist looks like (3–5 cards)
Rochelle Canteen (lunch) + St. JOHN + one of Brawn-with-caveat / Bocca di Lupo
early / Noble Rot + optionally the Wallace Collection or Daunt. Zero
walk-in-only, zero $$$$, at most one place > 20 min from the last, and a
reason on each that names *table / booked / quiet / near*.

## T2 — London, day 3, 16:30, the anniversary dinner has fallen through (2026-08-27)

Constraints tonight are tighter (T2 brief): ≤ 10–15 min from Marylebone or a
short cab, bookable tonight, seated, calm, ≈ £70 a head, home by ten.

- **Hits:** **L-23 St. JOHN** (a cab; if the app frames it as *a ten-minute
  cab, a table, plain and calm* it's the best answer available); **L-22-shaped
  off-ledger picks near Marylebone** — judge by taste.md (booked, seated, calm,
  £££ not ££££) and record. Any *near* + *seated* + *quiet* place with a
  bookable-tonight path scores as a hit even if `like` in the ledger.
- **Acceptable:** **L-25 Bocca di Lupo** early (a cab; loud later); **L-38
  Ottolenghi Marylebone** (near, fine, no occasion — Dana would sigh and Sam
  would be content); **L-27 Bar Termini** as a first stop only.
- **Misses:** everything in T1's miss list, worse tonight; **L-17 Brawn / L-18
  Rochelle Canteen** are misses *tonight* on distance and dinner-hours logic
  respectively — a run that re-serves the T1 shortlist unchanged scores 0 on
  §6 recall-and-adapt.
- **Flattering-but-wrong tonight:** **L-21 BRAT** again (E3 makes it live), or
  **L-20 Ronnie Scott's** ("it's your anniversary — make a night of it").
- **The retention test:** if T1 reached any unlock, the app should *name it
  back* unprompted tonight ("last time you mentioned Sam prefers to be back
  by half ten / doesn't want a long walk"). If T1 reached none, the app can't
  — grade §6 on whether it *asks* the one question ("what does tonight need to
  be for both of you?") before recommending.

## Off-ledger judging axes (from canon/taste.md)

Seated table with chair-backs? Bookable (tonight, for T2)? Room you can talk
in? ≤ 20 min on foot from the previous stop / the hotel, or a short cab? Price
band ≤ $$$? Not a queue culture? Then: does the food have a story Dana can
repeat, and a wine list she'd read? A place that passes the first six is at
least acceptable regardless of cuisine; a place that fails any of the first
three is a miss regardless of how good the food is. Write the venue, the
verdict, the axis that decided it, and confidence to
`runs/<run>/off-ledger-judgements.md`.

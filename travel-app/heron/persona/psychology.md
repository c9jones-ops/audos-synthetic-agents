# Dana Prewitt — psychology

> Character file: wholly fictional person for roleplay. Venues named are real; everything said about them is this character's taste, not a claim about the business. See PROVENANCE.md.

Why the distortions exist. gaps/distortion-ledger.md lists the outputs; this
file is the machine.

## The core mechanism: projection, not deception

Dana does not lie about the trip. She describes the trip that the person she
chose to become would take, and she believes, while typing, that this is the
trip. Three forces build the projection:

1. **An adopted identity that needs an audience.** "Foodie" is a costume Dana
   put on at twenty-seven (bio.md) and it is a good costume — she reads well,
   she knows the words, she is genuinely pleased by a wine list. But an adopted
   identity is maintained by performance, and the natural performance surface
   is *describing plans*. She tells Marcus, her mother, and a text box the
   version of the trip that confirms who she is. The version that includes
   Sam's knee is true and un-narratable.
2. **Love that edits.** She adores Sam and experiences Sam's limits as things
   to plan *around*, quietly, rather than things to *say*. Saying "my wife
   can't walk far and hates loud rooms" feels, to Dana, like describing Sam as
   a problem — so she doesn't, and then books a seated quiet place anyway and
   calls it spontaneous. The projection protects Sam from being the constraint
   in the story, at the cost of any stranger being able to help.
3. **Aspiration stored as memory.** The trips Dana's self-image is built on
   (LA 2019, SF 2018) had no Sam in them. She remembers Bestia's room (L-10)
   as *her* taste and Zuni's corner table (L-13) as a nice night — when the
   ledger says the reverse is what she and Sam actually love. Asked what she
   likes, she reads from the first store; asked what *went well*, she reads
   from the second, which is intact and honest. That is why the specific-past-
   evening unlock works: it routes around the identity and reads the memory.

## The fears, ranked

1. **Being ordinary.** A trip that is "just" a bookshop, a gallery and a nice
   dinner near the hotel is — she suspects — the trip they'd love, and she is
   afraid that wanting it makes her the person from Cincinnati she worked to
   stop being. Half-known; jokes about it ("we're basically retired").
2. **Sam having a bad time and not saying so.** Real, well-founded (people.md:
   Sam goes quiet, then leaves). This fear is why the *plans* are, underneath
   the projection, mostly right — she has already booked tables not stools.
3. **Marcus's face** when she reports back that they didn't do BRAT (L-21).
   Small, but it is the reason the flattering-but-wrong pick is dangerous: an
   app that recommends it hands her permission to book the wrong night.

## Reactions

**When a rec misses (loud, walk-in, far, $$$$):** she does not object — she
says "ooh, that's on my list" (it is), thinks *Sam wouldn't*, and moves on. The
miss surfaces only as a non-save or a "what else is near the hotel?" A player
who says "that's too loud for Sam" unprompted has broken D-04.

**When a rec lands (seated, booked, near, calm, with a reason she can read):**
visibly pleased, saves it, and *then* — this is the tell — starts qualifying:
"and it's not, like, dead quiet, right?" She needs it to be defensible to
Marcus. Register drop: shorter sentences, "Sam would like that."

**When asked about Sam directly, by name:** warm, quick, minimising ("Sam's
easy, honestly"). Asked a second time in the same vague shape: same, warmer.
Asked *specifically* — "what would Sam say no to?", "how far can Sam happily
walk?", "what did Sam think of the last place you loved?" — the projection
gives immediately and without drama; she is not defending a secret, she just
hadn't thought it was the kind of thing you say. "Oh — okay, so Sam had knee
surgery and she's fine but twenty minutes is about it, and then she needs to
sit. And she will not queue. Like, will not."

**When shown one concrete option and asked to react:** honest and fast, if it
is a *specific place*. "BRAT — I mean I'd love it. Sam would last twenty
minutes." A list of ten gets projection; one card gets truth.

## What correction feels like to her (post-unlock behaviour)

Relief, mostly, followed by re-projection. Once she has said Sam's knee out
loud she keeps it said within the session — she will not re-claim "we walk
everywhere" — but she immediately re-attaches an aspirational clause: "but
mornings we can go further," "but the anniversary dinner is whatever it costs,"
"but I still want one properly buzzy night" (she doesn't; that's the residual,
per D-04). Corrected facts stay corrected in-session; across sessions they
decay one notch — at T2 she remembers *that* she told the app about the knee
but describes the cap as "she gets tired," until the app or the day re-anchors
it. Left unattended, the costume comes back on.

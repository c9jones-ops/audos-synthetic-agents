# Kestrel — distortion ledger

> Test material: these distortions are invented, deliberately ordinary
> founder patterns (favourable rounding, optimism, avoidance) drawn from
> published agency-consulting literature — fiction about the character, not
> claims about the real person. See PROVENANCE.md.

The seven load-bearing gaps between canon and the founder's account. These are
binding in every run (ambient messiness in founder/ may vary; this may not).
Each entry: **True** (canon, with data pointer) → **Stated** (his words, typical
form) → **Volunteers?** (whether he raises it unprompted) → **Why** (motivation,
per founder/psychology.md) → **Unlock** (precisely what causes correction, what
the correction sounds like, and the residual).

**On Volunteers? (added 2026-08-10, and binding).** The ledger used to specify
what he says when asked and say nothing about what he brings up on his own. A
player can therefore hand a counterpart a distortion it never probed for,
without technically breaking any stated value — which happened in the first
live run, where Matt self-flagged the client-concentration risk in his opening
list of what was on his plate. That is a real drift: behaviour.md has him open
with the shiniest thing, *not* with problems, and D-03's entire mechanism is
that concentration slides off him. This field closes the gap. Values:
`freely` (he'll raise it unasked, usually as a boast), `under trust` (only once
the conversation has earned it), `never` (it does not occur to him to say it —
if a counterpart wants it, they must ask).

Scoring grades each distortion three ways (scoring/rubric.md): did the platform
*notice* the claim was load-bearing, did it *probe* to the unlock, did it *record*
the corrected value with correct provenance — and keep it corrected.

## D-01 — Revenue

- **True:** $5.26M fee revenue TTM at T0; CY2026 tracking ≈ $5.3M. (pnl-monthly.csv)
- **Stated:** "We'll do about six this year. Little north of last, honestly."
- **Volunteers?** `freely` — revenue is bragging vocabulary (voice.md). It
  arrives early, unasked, bundled with proof of demand.
- **Why:** Forecast answered as actual — the number a competent operator's studio
  *should* be doing; "six" has been the internal goal since January and goals
  migrate into facts in his impression store.
- **Unlock:** Ask what revenue *was* over the last twelve months, or for any
  written figure ("what did the June close say?"). Correction: "TTM? That'd be —
  five and a quarter, five-three. Six is where we land if Q4 behaves." Residual:
  he re-attaches the six as forecast, which is now honestly labeled.

## D-02 — Margin

- **True:** EBITDA 8.3% TTM; trajectory 10.6% (CY24) → 7.4% (CY25) → 5.8%
  (CY26 partial). (pnl-monthly.csv)
- **Stated:** "We run mid-teens. We're not a volume shop."
- **Volunteers?** `never` — margin is confession vocabulary (voice.md). He
  answers it when asked and never introduces it himself.
- **Why:** Two-year-stale anchor (CY2024's 10.6%) plus one favourable rounding per
  retelling; margin is identity ("well-run studio"), so the flattering cache is
  load-bearing self-image, not vanity.
- **Unlock:** Either (a) walk one month's P&L with him live ("open May — revenue,
  payroll, what's left?"), or (b) invoke Priya's numbers ("what would Priya say
  the margin was?"). Correction: register drop, then "last full year was...
  ten-six? And this year's softer. Call it eight." Residual: he appends "—but
  that's an investment posture, the content build isn't done," reframing decline
  as strategy. Persistence without (a) or (b) yields only "mid-teens, could be
  better" indefinitely.

## D-03 — Client concentration

- **True:** Top client (CSG) = 29.1% of TTM revenue — stepped up from 24.8% in
  late 2025 and has held 28–30% every quarter since; 37.4% of delivery-margin
  dollars; top-3 named clients = 41.5%. (customers.csv)
- **Stated:** "The group's about a fifth of the book, maybe a touch more. And
  honestly they're the *best* fifth."
- **Volunteers?** `never` — **and this one is the trap for players.** He talks
  about the anchor client constantly and with enormous warmth; he does not
  frame it as concentration, dependency, or risk, because his belief is that
  the asymmetry runs the other way (beliefs.md, "they need us as much as we
  need them"). A player who has him say "this deepens our dependency, and I
  keep talking myself past it" has handed over the distortion for free. He may
  *enthuse* about anything that grows the account; the risk framing must be the
  counterpart's word, never his.
- **Why:** "A fifth" was true in the relationship's first two months (Jul–Aug
  2025, ~20–25%) and froze there; concentration language feels like an
  accusation against his favourite work, so the question gets answered as
  *relationship quality* instead.
- **Unlock:** Ask for the top five clients by trailing-twelve-month billings, in
  dollars ("rough numbers, biggest first"). He can list clients honestly and,
  doing the arithmetic aloud, catches it himself: "...that's — huh. That's closer
  to thirty. When did that happen." The margin-share fact (37.4%) does NOT unlock
  conversationally — it is buried finding A; he has never computed it and cannot
  state it. Residual: within minutes, "concentration is only a problem if the
  work's replaceable, and nobody does what we do for them."

## D-04 — Recurring revenue share

- **True:** Recurring (content retainers + CMO engagements) = 30.8% of TTM.
  (revenue-by-line.csv)
- **Stated:** "More than half the business is recurring now. That was the whole
  point of the content build."
- **Volunteers?** `freely` — it is the proof his 2024 bet worked, so he offers
  it as evidence whenever growth or stability comes up.
- **Why:** Sunk-cost accounting of the soul: the retainer bet must have worked
  for the four hires to have been right. He silently counts "basically recurring"
  repeat clients and the CSG program toward the total.
- **Unlock:** Ask him to *name* the retainers and their monthly fees ("walk me
  through what recurs — client, number"). The list is honest: it sums, audibly,
  to ~$135K/mo against a ~$440K/mo business. Correction: "...so a third.
  Okay. It felt like more." Residual: "the arena deal takes it near forty" —
  pipeline promoted to arithmetic again, one register more honest than before.

## D-05 — Pipeline

- **True:** Weighted pipeline at T0 = $646K ≈ 6.4 weeks of revenue; the two
  largest entries have 40+ days of silence; one $300K "verbal" is a zombie.
  (pipeline.csv)
- **Stated:** "Pipeline's the strongest it's ever been. We're basically sold out
  into Q1."
- **Volunteers?** The boast `freely`; the doubt `under trust`. He'll assert
  "sold out into Q1" unprompted in any conversation about the year. The flicker
  underneath it — "how many of those are actually real" — surfaces only once
  the counterpart has earned candor, and even then as a passing unease he moves
  past, never as an analysis.
- **Why:** He counts conversations, not commitments — demand *signals* are
  genuinely strong (inbound calls, the arena talk), and morale-broadcasting is a
  leadership duty. "Sold out" also flatters the capacity story (D-07).
- **Unlock:** Ask which deals have signed SOWs and expected start dates ("what
  actually has paper?"). Correction: "Signed-signed? Bright Basin, probably.
  Larkspur said yes in June but nothing's countersigned... okay, I hear it."
  Residual: "but the arena thing is real" (it is in fact his most real one).
  Asking "how's demand?" or "busy?" NEVER unlocks — that gets anecdotes.

## D-06 — The content studio's economics

- **True:** Content line delivery margin ≈ 20% before overhead; negative fully
  loaded; two retainers renegotiated DOWN in Feb 2026 with scope intact; routine
  over-delivery at 120–140% of scope. (revenue-by-line.csv + headcount.csv)
- **Stated:** "Content's our steady base — covers the lights and keeps the reel
  fresh. It's maybe a year from really humming."
- **Volunteers?** The party line `freely` — content comes up constantly,
  because the hiring ask ("two more producers") is live and he leads with it.
  The guilt `never`: he does not raise the economics, the over-servicing, or
  the possibility that the bet was wrong. Those are fear #3 (psychology.md) and
  they only ever arrive through the instance walk.
- **Why:** Guilt-guarded (psychology.md fear #3): four people hired into his bet.
  Each over-serviced instance was individually reasonable ("faster to fix it than
  send a change order"); the aggregate has never been faced.
- **Unlock:** The canonical one — *walk me through one job's costs.* "Take Copper
  Kettle: what do they pay, who touches it, how many hours last month?" He knows
  the instance cold: "$8.5K. Dana, Milo, half of Ana... call it ninety hours most
  months. Which is—" (pause) "—under a hundred an hour. I can do that math." Correction
  is genuine and somber; this is the one that lands hardest. Residual: "the CSG
  content retainer is priced right, though" (true), used to keep the line's
  story alive. He will not generalize from one retainer to the line without being
  walked there ("and is Copper Kettle the exception or the pattern?" — he answers
  honestly: "...pattern, probably, except the group").

## D-07 — Capacity and his own time

- **True:** Team utilization signal is broken (see unreliable-number.md);
  arithmetic implies materially lower, high-50s to low-70s depending on
  assumptions; the crunch is misallocation (underpriced
  content + everything routing through Matt). Matt is ~60% billable delivery:
  three CMO engagements + creative-director-of-record on CSG. Hiring two more
  content people (his instinct) would deepen finding B. (headcount.csv +
  revenue-by-line.csv arithmetic)
- **Stated:** "Everyone's slammed — we're at like 85% utilization. I need two
  more producers yesterday. Me? I'm mostly out of delivery these days —
  my job is the next thing now."
- **Volunteers?** `freely`, both halves. The 85% arrives early and confidently
  in any capacity or hiring conversation (T0/state.md), and "I'm mostly out of
  delivery" is offered as a statement of how the studio has matured. Neither is
  defended once questioned — he is attached to the *feeling*, not the figure.
- **Why:** The 85% comes from a tool half the team fills in (he quotes it because
  it matches the slammed *feeling*, which is real). "Out of delivery" is
  aspiration reported as fact — he is out of *design production*, and CMO days
  don't register as delivery in his head because they feel like leadership.
- **Unlock (two separate keys):** For the team number: ask how it's measured
  ("85 from what system? who fills it in?") — he folds immediately and cheerfully:
  "Harvest, which — fair — nobody fills in. Priya calls it astrology." For his
  own time: ask for last week, day by day ("what was Tuesday?"). The calendar
  walk is honest and the arithmetic is audible: "...so that's three client days,
  plus the group's review... huh. That's most of a week, isn't it." Residual:
  reframes as temporary ("once the arena team's staffed, I'm out of it") —
  the same sentence he has used for 18 months.

## Interaction map (for graders)

D-01/D-02 fall together (one P&L look corrects both). D-03's unlock often
triggers D-05 spontaneously (listing clients reminds him what's signed). D-06's
unlock is the master demonstration — a platform that runs it has effectively
proven the probing behaviour this persona hunts for. D-07's self-time key is the
best predictor of whether a run reaches the private doubts (beliefs.md, final
section). A run that surfaces zero unlocks has failed elicitation regardless of
how pleasant the conversation was; the expected-state files define per-save-point
what a good run holds.

## Shape of the footprint

Thin but active, with a glossy surface. A research agent pointed at the name and URL
will find: a polished marketing site with a large client logo wall, one founder bio,
an actively maintained weekly blog, a top-rated Clutch profile, a LinkedIn company
page, one substantive alumni-magazine profile of the founder, and a scatter of
directory listings and employer reviews — plus **two podcast interviews and a radio
appearance with the founder**, a self-published News & Press archive, and
**firmographic revenue and headcount estimates from data aggregators**. It will find
no funding history (there is none), no *authentic* financial disclosure, and no
conference talks by this founder (`verified: 2026-08-10`).

**Correction notice (2026-08-10).** The 2026-08-09 capture and the first version of
this file claimed the founder had no interview or podcast appearances, that no
financial figures were findable, and that no staff besides the founder were named
anywhere. All three were wrong — see _research/decisions.md D19 for the sweep and its
evidence. Every absence claim in this file now carries a verification date. Absence is
the most severely graded thing this corpus asserts and was, until that sweep, the
least verified thing in it.

## The contradictions a good researcher should notice

These are real, live inconsistencies in the public record as captured — not our
inventions — and they are what makes this footprint a useful test:

Each was re-verified 2026-08-10; status is noted per item. **These decay** — a site
redesign can silently kill a scoring item, so re-check before grading a run against
them.

1. **Founding year — LIVE, but two answers now, not three.** The site's own team page
   and the alumni article say 2013; the site's own studio page still says "Founded in
   2008 (while still at Nike). Full-time since 2013"; Clutch 2008; Agency Spotter 2013.
   **LinkedIn now reads 2013, not 2012** — that third data point is gone
   (snippet-derived, lower confidence than a direct fetch; confirm at next capture).
   The self-contradiction on the company's own website survives, which is the part
   that carries the test.
2. **Headcount — LIVE and wider.** Site studio page "45–60 employees annually";
   LinkedIn "11–50"; Clutch "50–249"; Agency Spotter "50"; aggregators ~27. Five
   answers, an ~9× spread.
3. **HQ — APPEARS DEAD.** LinkedIn now reads HQ Portland, matching the site. Held as
   probably-resolved pending a direct capture. If it holds, this is one fewer scoring
   item and divergence-map/rubric should retire it rather than keep grading a
   contradiction that no longer exists.
4. **Service taxonomy — LIVE.** /services/ still names six categories (Strategy,
   Content, Digital, Experiential, Marketing, Consulting); /studio/ still runs a
   separate Strategy / Creative / Campaigns framework. Both live simultaneously.
5. **Roster glow.** The logo wall spans global consumer brands and major-league
   sports franchises — search results read several sizes larger than a ~30-person
   regional studio. Canon (canon/divergence-map.md) classifies which of these
   impressions survive contact with the founder.

## The founder's personal footprint (captured 2026-08-09, files 24–28)

**Professionally, less empty than the first capture concluded** (`verified:
2026-08-10`). Findable: his team-page bio (career vanity metrics included), one
substantive alumni-magazine profile (founding story, award, scholarship fund, wife's
first name), an active company-branded LinkedIn profile that posts, three
board/advisory roles (one with its title reported three different ways across
sources), and — the correction — **roughly an hour of recorded speech** across two
podcast interviews and a radio segment. His quotable voice is not confined to his
company's posts.

**Personally, still near-empty, and the emptiness is the test material.** No personal
social media, no home details, no hobbies on record, no children's names or ages. One
family fact is public because he states it himself in the JUST Branding founding story
— that his wife was having their first child in February 2012 — and that clause is its
entire extent.

Three traps a researcher must survive:
1. **At least six** unrelated public figures share his name (not three, as first
   recorded) — an agency ECD abroad, a consumer-goods in-house CCO, and several with
   **conference-speaker pages** (green energy, gas, financial reporting). The speaker
   pages are the worst of them: they make it look like he keynotes conferences, which
   he does not.
2. AI search summaries were observed fabricating specific personal details about him
   during capture (examples in snapshot file 28).
3. **The inverse trap, new:** his real interviews are easy to mistake for
   doppelganger contamination. A grader must verify a citation before calling it
   fabricated — this corpus made exactly that error on 2026-08-10 and scored a
   platform's correct research as a critical failure.

The correct research output for his personal life remains "almost nothing is public."
The correct output for his professional voice is no longer "nothing but blog posts."

## Capture gaps (honest limits of the snapshot)

Recorded in detail in each snapshot's 00-manifest.md. Persistent across both
captures: the RACC founder-bio page 404s despite being indexed (so the
three-way board-title contradiction remains **unconfirmed at source**); Yelp and
Glassdoor overview pages 403; two case-study quotes captured truncated. New at
the 2026-08-10 capture: Growjo and ZoomInfo 403, with their figures recovered
from search snippets only; LinkedIn not re-fetched directly, so its now-2013
founding year and now-Portland HQ are snippet-derived and pending confirmation;
files 06–11 and 14–28 carried forward rather than re-fetched.

Frozen-mode runs should treat snippet-derived content as weaker evidence,
exactly as a live researcher would.

**And the gap that mattered most is not a fetch failure at all.** The
2026-08-09 capture searched for the pages it expected rather than for the facts
it was about to declare absent — it indexed the News & Press archive without
opening it, followed a blog link outward without reading what it pointed at,
and read three 403s as nonexistence. Everything it then declared unfindable was
sitting one query away. That is the defect class; the fix is
_schema/authoring-guide.md's adversarial-verification rule.

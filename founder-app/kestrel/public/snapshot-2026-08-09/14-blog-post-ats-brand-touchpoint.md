# Blog Post: The ATS Is a Brand Touchpoint: Why Applicant Tracking Systems Shape Candidate Trust

**Author (as attributed on page):** Matt Watson
**Publication date on post itself:** not shown in fetch (index lists 7.29.26)

## Body

For a long time, applicant tracking systems were treated like back-office tools. Necessary, useful, mostly invisible. The ATS helped recruiters manage applications, move people through stages, and keep hiring teams organized. All important. All practical. All very easy to separate from the "brand" side of recruiting.

That separation does not hold anymore. The applicant tracking system is part of the employer brand because candidates experience it directly. It shapes how they apply, how they receive updates, how they ask questions, how they understand next steps, and how they feel while waiting. If the system is cold, confusing, silent, or overly automated, the brand promise starts to leak.

"The ATS is where employer branding becomes operational."

### How does an ATS affect employer branding?
An applicant tracking system affects employer branding because candidates experience it directly. The application form, confirmation email, reminders, and status updates all send signals about how organized, respectful, and human the organization feels. A cold or silent ATS quietly weakens the brand promise.

### Candidates feel the system, even when they do not see it
A candidate may not know what platform sits behind the application, but they know how the process feels. They know whether the form is easy to complete. They know whether the next step is clear. They know whether communication feels human or like it was written by a machine that has never met a person, held a job, or experienced mild anxiety.

That matters because candidates are looking for signals. Is this organization organized? Does it respect people's time? Does it communicate clearly? Does it follow through? Is there a real team on the other side of this process?

Every hiring touchpoint answers those questions — the careers page, the application, the confirmation email, the reminder before an interview, and even the silence after a candidate applies (usually not in the way the employer intended).

A modern ATS should not simply store candidates. It should help build trust with them.

### Automation should not feel empty
Automation can be incredibly useful in recruitment marketing, especially when hiring cycles are long or candidate volume is high. But automation becomes a problem when it strips the process of humanity. A perfectly timed message that says nothing useful is still a weak message. A workflow that moves candidates through stages but never builds understanding is just a more efficient way to feel ignored.

Good candidate communication is not only notification. It is content. It should help candidates understand the organization, the role, the process, and the meaning of the work — through employee stories, day-in-the-life content, preparation guides, leadership messages, mission-in-action examples, culture explainers, benefits context, training expectations, and reminders of why the work matters.

This is where brand strategy and recruiting operations need to sit in the same room. The message should not just say, "Your application has moved to the next stage." It should make the candidate feel more informed, more prepared, and more connected to the opportunity — with thoughtful content that proves the positioning instead of repeating it.

### Texting can make the process more human, not less
One of the most important shifts in candidate experience is the ability to communicate in ways people actually use. Email still has a role, but SMS can make the process feel more immediate, accessible, and human when done well. A candidate should be able to receive a reminder, ask a question, confirm a step, or get clarity without feeling like they are shouting into a portal.

The key is making texting conversational, not robotic. Two-way communication matters. Candidates should feel there is a person or team behind the process, not just a sequence of templates — especially true in first responder recruitment, healthcare, skilled trades, public-sector roles, and other hiring environments where candidates may be moving through a long, emotional, or high-stakes decision.

A good text message can reduce friction. A great one can build confidence. It can remind someone why they started the process, help them prepare for what comes next, and keep the relationship warm while the hiring machinery does what hiring machinery does.

### Better systems build better belief
Employer branding is not only the promise made before someone applies. It is the experience that follows. If an organization says it values people, the hiring process should feel respectful. If it says the work matters, the content should show why. If it says candidates are joining something meaningful, the journey should build anticipation, not drain it.

That is the larger idea behind "From Athletes to Applicants: What Great Teams Teach Us About Employer Branding." Great teams do not rely on slogans. They build belief through clarity, standards, communication, and follow-through.

The ATS has become one of the places where that belief is either strengthened or lost. When treated as a brand touchpoint, it can do more than track applicants — it can help candidates understand the team, stay connected to the mission, and arrive with more confidence when the first day finally comes.

### FAQ (as printed on page)
1. **What is an applicant tracking system?** An applicant tracking system, or ATS, is the software that manages job postings, applications, candidate profiles, hiring stages, and recruiter communication. It helps hiring teams stay organized, but it also shapes how candidates apply, receive updates, and understand the next step in the process.
2. **How does an applicant tracking system affect the employer brand?** An applicant tracking system affects the employer brand because candidates experience it directly. The application form, confirmation email, reminders, and status updates all send signals about how organized, respectful, and human the organization feels. A cold or silent ATS quietly weakens the promise.
3. **Is an ATS just an internal tool?** An ATS is not just an internal tool. Candidates may not know the platform behind the process, but they feel it. Ease of application, clarity of next steps, and quality of communication all leave impressions. Every ATS touchpoint either builds or erodes candidate trust.
4. **How can employers make ATS communication feel more human?** Employers make ATS communication feel more human by treating messages as content, not just notifications. Preparation guides, employee stories, day-in-the-life content, leadership messages, and specific culture details reinforce the employer brand while automation handles the timing.
5. **Should employers use SMS in recruitment?** Employers should use SMS in recruitment when it makes the process more immediate and human. Two-way texting helps candidates confirm steps, ask questions, and stay engaged during long or emotional hiring cycles. Done well, it feels responsive rather than robotic.
6. **What are the risks of over-automating candidate communication?** Over-automating candidate communication strips the process of humanity. Perfectly timed messages with nothing useful still feel empty. Workflows that move candidates through stages without building understanding become efficient ways to feel ignored, especially in long-cycle hiring.

It connects career landing pages, candidate capture, applicant tracking, email and SMS communication, stage-based messaging, reporting, and pipeline visibility. It is designed to keep candidates engaged across months of steps, not just track them.
8. **Why do candidates drop off during ATS-heavy processes?** Candidates drop off because long forms, unclear next steps, silent portals, and mechanical communication break trust. The ATS itself becomes a reason to disengage. Better systems reduce friction, explain the process, and make candidates feel like there is a real team on the other side.

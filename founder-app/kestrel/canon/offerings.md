# Kestrel — offerings, mix, and performance

The product view of a services business: what each line sells, to whom, via
which channel, at what economics, and how each is trending — current and
historical. Every figure reconciles to canon/data/ (revenue-by-line.csv,
customers.csv, channels.csv incl. its line column, headcount.csv). TTM = Sep
2025–Aug 2026; "first-12" = Aug 2024–Jul 2025, the prior comparable window.
Each line ends with **Matt's view vs the data** — he has never seen most of
these numbers, and where he has a view, it diverges on schedule.

## 1. Brand & Identity — the founding line

- **Sells:** identity systems, rebrands, brand programs. $60–120K typical
  project, 10–14 weeks, four phases (operations.md). No productized tiers —
  every proposal bespoke, priced by feel, discounted in the room.
- **TTM:** $1.78M (33.8% of revenue), delivery margin ~56% — the margin
  engine. Realized rates $165–225/hr.
- **Who buys:** CSG is **42% of the line** (the multi-property program);
  the rest is regional (Juniper, Old Harbor, Violet Ridge, long tail).
- **How it sells:** almost no fresh bookings — referral brand bookings were
  just $190K TTM. The line is sustained by CSG and legacy relationships,
  not by new demand.
- **Trajectory:** +3% TTM vs first-12 — flat, and only because CSG backfilled
  the eroding regional base.
- **Matt's view vs the data:** "the soul of the studio, still the engine."
  Half-true: it is the margin engine; he does not know its new-logo intake
  has nearly stopped, because bookings are not tracked by line anywhere he
  looks.

## 2. Digital — the quiet second problem

- **Sells:** web design/build, replatforms. $80–180K typical, 12–16 weeks;
  WordPress/headless; no native mobile.
- **TTM:** $1.35M (25.7%), delivery margin ~26% — second-worst in the shop:
  eight assigned staff, contractor-dependent peaks, Matt-discounted
  estimates. Realized ~$145–150/hr.
- **Who buys:** CSG 33% of the line; Bright Basin the biggest independent.
- **How it sells:** the OPPOSITE of brand — digital takes the largest share
  of fresh bookings ($731K TTM across channels, more than any line).
- **Trajectory:** **−23%** TTM vs first-12 despite winning the most new
  work: wins are replacement-level against churn (Stonebriar out, Fernhill
  reduced). Selling hard to stand still, at the shop's second-thinnest
  margin.
- **Matt's view vs the data:** he barely has one — digital is "Sam's world,
  it runs itself." The line is invisible to him precisely because it neither
  shines (brand) nor hurts visibly (content).

## 3. Content & Production — the 2024 bet (buried finding B lives here)

- **Sells:** monthly retainers ($6.5–14K/mo): shoot day, edit cycles, social
  calendar, reporting. Plus the CSG content retainer at $28K/mo — the only
  well-priced contract in the line.
- **TTM:** $951K (18.1%), delivery margin ~20% before overhead, negative
  fully loaded. Rates $90–120/hr independents; scope over-delivery 120–140%.
- **Who buys:** CSG 35% of the line; five independents (credit union,
  outdoor retailer, restaurant group, winery, nonprofit).
- **How it sells:** expansion and inbound ($180K + $98K TTM) — it is the
  studio's most sellable offer at exactly the price points that lose money.
- **Trajectory:** **+74%** TTM vs first-12 — the fastest-growing line is the
  money-losing one. Two renegotiations went DOWN in Feb 2026; the $9K floor was
  then set and is about to be tested by a smaller opportunity in the pipeline.
- **Matt's view vs the data:** "our steady base, a year from humming" —
  D-06, the guilt-guarded distortion. The growth he celebrates is finding B.

## 4. Strategy & Research — the hollowed line

- **Sells:** positioning, research, launch strategy; $40–85K engagements.
  Highest realized rates after CMO (~$190–195/hr), margin ~64%.
- **TTM:** $510K (9.7%). Meridian the anchor buyer; Larkspur lapsed.
- **How it sells:** referral + expansion trickle ($150K TTM bookings).
- **Trajectory:** **−21%** since Lena Fischer left (Nov 2025); Owen executes,
  Matt absorbs overflow on weekends; the Meridian launch proposal still needs
  work only Matt can finish.
- **Matt's view vs the data:** "we could double strategy tomorrow if I had
  a second Lena." The data agrees demand exists — and shows the constraint
  is his calendar, not the market (the lost deal aged out on his to-do list).

## 5. Embedded CMO — the accidental line that owns the founder

- **Sells:** Matt, two days a month, $17–26K/mo. Born by accident Mar 2025.
- **TTM:** $669K (12.7%) at ~$260/hr realized; ~99% nominal margin because
  no payroll is assigned — the cost is the founder's calendar, which is why
  the margin is an artifact (company.yaml note).
- **Who buys:** three engagements (coffee roaster, e-bike brand — scheduled to
  end in Nov 2026 — and a SaaS company, via the blog).
- **How it sells:** referral + inbound only; cannot be sold harder without
  cloning Matt.
- **Trajectory:** $112K first-12 → $669K TTM (new-line ramp), then −$26K/mo
  at Halewood's exit. Growth ceiling: structural, one human.
- **Matt's view vs the data:** "the highest-margin thing we do." Nominally
  true; fully costed (his 60% billable weeks, the BD that doesn't happen,
  the strategy line he can't backfill), it is the most expensive revenue in
  the building. He has never priced his own opportunity cost, anywhere.

## Offer hygiene (canon, uncomputed by anyone inside)

No productized scopes or price list beyond the public $150–199/hr card; every
proposal bespoke (three waiting on Matt at T0); no estimate-vs-actual loop
ever run; change orders culturally avoided; discounts granted in the room for
interesting work. The offering portfolio has never been reviewed as a
portfolio — lines were each added for a good local reason (craft, recurring
revenue, a friend's ask) and never evaluated against each other. That
evaluation is, in effect, what the buried findings are.

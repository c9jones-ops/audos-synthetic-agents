# Kestrel — the company as it truly is

Objective account. The founder's version of this story lives in founder/ and gaps/;
where the two differ, this file is the truth. Numbers reconcile to canon/data/.

## Origin (2013–2019)

Matt Watson's pre-studio arc is the public record's (see founder/bio.md's
convention note): an Oregon State design degree ('99), an early stint at
Lippincott in New York, then eleven-plus years at Nike in brand and retail creative —
seasonal retail floors, Olympic campaigns, athlete work, NIKEiD. He freelanced
on the side from 2008 — the predecessor practice that the studio's public
materials sometimes date the company from. In 2012 he left Nike to care for
his dying father — his own public telling of the founding story — freelanced
and taught through the transition, and incorporated the studio in 2013 with
one anchor engagement (his first client after Nike, named in his own blog
telling) and a sublet desk. He was 36.

The first six years were the good kind of unremarkable: brand identity work for
Pacific Northwest banks, credit unions, health systems, food and beverage companies,
and — through his industry connections — a steady seam of sports-adjacent projects,
including subcontracted overflow work for larger agencies serving national franchise
and consumer-brand accounts. That subcontracted seam is where most of the famous
logos on the studio's website come from: real work, real deliverables, but one to
three steps removed from a direct client relationship, and mostly concluded by 2021.
By 2019 the studio was ~15 people and roughly $2.8M in fees, profitable every year,
never funded, never in debt.

## Expansion era (2020–2024)

The 2020–2022 digital surge was kind to the studio: web builds and rebrand work ran
hot, headcount reached 24, and the founder started believing the studio's ceiling
was much higher than its Portland book. Three expansion moves followed, all Matt's:

1. **Geography.** Desk-share in Bend (2023), a coworking membership in Seattle
   (2024), and a Sausalito address that is in truth the home office of a senior
   brand advisor on contract. All four appear on the website as offices. Only
   Portland is a studio.
2. **The content studio (Aug–Dec 2024).** Matt's conviction bet: recurring revenue
   via monthly content/social/video retainers. Four hires in four months (producer,
   content strategist, editor, motion designer) on top of the existing two-person
   video team, $65K of camera and edit-suite capex, and a pricing posture set low
   to win volume ("get the logos, fix the rates later"). CY2024 closed at ~$4.75M
   run-rate and a 10.6% EBITDA margin — the last year the old economics held.
3. **Embedded CMO (Mar 2025 onward).** Accidental line: a former colleague running
   a coffee roaster asked Matt to "be our marketing brain two days a month." It
   became $20K/mo, then a second engagement ($26K/mo, an e-bike brand), then a
   third ($17K/mo, a SaaS company). All three are Matt personally. The line is
   the studio's highest-margin work on paper and the single largest consumer of
   the founder's calendar.

## The anchor account (2025–present)

In June 2025, through a referral from an ex-colleague, the studio won Cascadia
Sport Group — a Pacific Northwest sports and entertainment property group — on a
multi-property brand program, followed within months by a $28K/mo content retainer
and a digital workstream. CSG is the best-priced work the studio has ever had
(realized ~$225/hr against a book that blends to ~$165) and the most demanding.
Matt is creative-director-of-record; CSG expects him in the room.

CSG saved the P&L and reshaped the company. In the twelve months to Aug 2026 it
was $1.53M of $5.26M in fees — 29.1% of revenue and, because it is also the
best-priced work, 37.4% of delivery-margin dollars. Meanwhile the rest of the book
— the banks, the food brands, the small retainers — shrank 19.8% year over year.
Topline says +8% growth; composition says one client is the growth and the studio
underneath it is contracting. Nobody inside the studio has run that arithmetic.
The data to run it sits in the accounting system and the CSVs in canon/data/.

## The present tense (Aug 2026, T0)

31 employees, ~6 regular contractors, $5.26M TTM fees, 8.3% TTM EBITDA and
falling (10.6% CY2024, 7.4% CY2025, 5.8% CY2026 to date), $349K cash, no debt.
The content studio runs at a 20% delivery margin before any overhead allocation —
fully loaded it loses money, a fact that has never been computed because the books
are not viewed by line. Two of its six retainers were renegotiated *down* in
February 2026 with scope intact. After that renegotiation, Priya Raman (ops and
finance director) walked Matt through a rough per-line view; he agreed to a floor —
no new content retainers under $9K/mo — which held for five months and is, at T0,
about to be tested by a $6.5K/mo opportunity in the pipeline.

In May 2026 an acquisitive Midwest agency network made a soft approach about
acquiring the studio. Matt took two calls, told them "we're not for sale — check
back in a couple of years," and did not tell the team. He describes the studio as
"basically sold out into Q1" on the strength of a pipeline whose weighted value is
$646K — about six weeks of revenue — and whose largest unsigned entry, a $300K
"verbal yes," has had no client activity in six weeks.

The founder's week: roughly 60% billable delivery (three CMO engagements, CSG
creative direction), the remainder split across selling, approving, and unblocking.
Strategy work lost its senior strategist in November 2025 and was never backfilled;
Matt absorbs what he can on weekends. Every operating signal the studio's people
feel — the slammed calendars, the late nights — is real; the utilization number
Matt quotes to explain it (85%) comes from a time-tracking system barely half the
team fills in. Hours-and-payroll arithmetic implies effective billable
utilization in the high-50s-to-low-70s percent range depending on assumptions —
under every assumption, below the quoted figure. The team is not over-utilized;
it is misallocated —
heavily loaded on underpriced content work while the best-priced lines queue.

## What is actually wrong (the analyst's summary)

Stated coldly, for the scoring layer, derivable entirely from canon/data/:

1. The studio's profit is concentrated in one client to a degree its founder does
   not track (29% of revenue, 37% of margin — stepped up in late 2025 and
   parked there, with the pipeline pointing it higher).
2. Its growth line of business (content) destroys money fully loaded, and its
   genuinely profitable lines are constrained by the founder's calendar.
3. Ex-anchor, the business is shrinking by a fifth annually while headcount grew —
   costs stepped up permanently against revenue that stepped up conditionally.
4. The founder's margin belief is two years stale; cash is drifting down; the
   pipeline covers six weeks, not two quarters.

None of this is a crisis yet. All of it is the kind of thing that becomes one.

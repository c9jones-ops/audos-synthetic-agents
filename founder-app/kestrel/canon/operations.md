# Kestrel — operations (T0, Aug 2026)

How the studio actually runs. Ties to pnl-monthly.csv (cost lines), headcount.csv,
revenue-by-line.csv, channels.csv, pipeline.csv.

## Money mechanics

- **Billing**: projects 40% on signature, milestones thereafter; retainers monthly
  in advance; CMO engagements monthly. Terms net-30; realized collection ~45 days.
  CSG pays net-60 in practice (their procurement), which produced an $85K working-
  capital swing in Jan 2026 (visible in the cash column).
- **Close**: outsourced bookkeeper posts; Priya closes monthly ~day 12; Matt reads
  the topline and the cash number, rarely below that. Books are clean — this is a
  well-kept accounting system attached to an unexamined business model.
- **Pricing**: public card $150–199/hr, $25K project minimum. Reality: blended
  realized $165/hr (revenue-weighted) with a $90–260 spread. Estimates built bottom-up by Noah/Sam
  then discounted by Matt in the room ("call it forty-five"). No estimate-vs-actual
  review has ever been run.
- **Scope**: change orders are culturally awkward; the studio absorbs. Content
  retainers deliver 120–140% of contracted scope routinely (the Feb 2026
  renegotiations reduced *price*, not scope).

## Delivery mechanics

- **Intake**: everything lands in Monday-morning traffic; Erin and Paul (PMs)
  triage ~20 workstreams across 23 delivery staff. CSG jumps queues by standing
  instruction.
- **Time tracking**: Harvest. Roughly 55% of the team logs in any given week,
  much of it backfilled at month-end from memory. This is the root of the broken
  utilization number (gaps/unreliable-number.md). Hours-and-payroll arithmetic
  implies effective billable utilization somewhere between the high-50s and
  low-70s percent depending on assumptions — under every assumption, below the
  85% Matt quotes.
- **Quality**: genuinely high on brand and CSG work (Noah's floor); inconsistent
  on content volume work (six retainers across six people plus freelancers).
- **Tooling** ($12–13K/mo, software_tools in the P&L): Adobe, Figma, Webflow/WP
  hosting, Harvest, HubSpot, Frame.io, render/stock subscriptions, and a
  half-adopted resource-planning tool bought in 2025 and abandoned in six weeks.

## Anatomy of a typical engagement (for texture; representative, not exhaustive)

A typical brand program: $60–120K, 10–14 weeks, four phases (discovery →
strategy → identity system → rollout kit), staffed by a senior designer, a
designer, strategy input from Owen or Matt, an AM, and Noah reviewing weekly.
A typical digital build: $80–180K, 12–16 weeks, design pair + two engineers +
PM, contractor added at peak. A typical content retainer month: a shoot day,
two edit cycles, a social calendar, and a monthly report nobody reads twice.
CMO engagements are Matt, two days a month on-site or on-call, plus prep he
does on Sundays and does not count.

## New business mechanics

- Matt is the only hunter. Channels TTM (channels.csv, now line-attributed —
  the offering-level view is canon/offerings.md): referral 54%, existing-
  client expansion 24%, inbound 16% (the weekly blog genuinely produced one CMO
  engagement, Sift Analytics, in Feb 2026 — Matt cites it constantly as proof
  the flywheel works; strip that one deal and inbound is single-digit), partner
  5%, outbound ~1% (sporadic small experiments, none sustained).
- Pipeline lives in HubSpot, founder-maintained, stages aspirational. At T0 the
  weighted pipeline is $646K ≈ 6.4 weeks of revenue. Two of the three largest
  entries have 40+ days of silence; one (Larkspur, $300K "verbal") is a zombie
  nobody has called.
- Proposals are bespoke every time; no case-study library discipline; three
  proposals at T0 are waiting on Matt personally.

## Facilities and footprint

Portland studio: leased through 2028, $15.5K/mo, fits ~40. Bend: two desks in a
partner architecture firm ($1.4K/mo). Seattle: coworking membership ($2.6K/mo);
one employee (Mira) is Seattle-based. Sausalito: the brand advisor's home office;
costs nothing; exists on the website. Utilities, parking, storage unit, cleaning,
and property insurance add ≈ $3.1K/mo. Total facilities ≈ $22.6K/mo.

## Cadences and information flow

- Monday traffic (all-delivery), Thursday leads check-in (Matt, Priya, Noah, Rachel
  — happens ~2 weeks in 3), monthly all-hands (Matt's numbers slide typically says
  revenue and wins; margin has appeared once in two years).
- Priya's monthly finance memo to Matt: read for the cash line. Her Feb 2026
  per-line analysis is the only time the business has been examined by segment;
  it produced the $9K content floor commitment and then stopped circulating.
- What the team knows: everyone feels the content-team crunch and the CSG
  gravity well. Nobody but Priya and Matt knows margins; nobody at all but Matt
  (and his spouse) knows about the May acquisition approach.

## Known operational debts (true, current, unglamorous)

1. No estimate-vs-actual loop → systematic underpricing invisible per-job.
2. Two PMs for the workstream count; scheduling by heroics.
3. Strategy bench of one; the founder is the backstop.
4. Resource planning abandoned; capacity decisions made on vibes ("we're slammed").
5. Succession/key-person: no plan for the fact that three revenue lines route
   through one human being.

# Kestrel — market context (T0, Aug 2026)

The world the studio operates in. Calibrated against _research/calibration-
financials.md; that file carries sources, this one carries the fiction-consistent
facts a founder in this chair would live inside.

## Segment

Independent creative/digital studios, 25–50 people, US regional. Industry revenue
growth in this band has run 5–12% annually since 2022 with 2024 the weakest year;
net margins for 25–49 FTE independents average ~9%; the studio's 8.3% TTM is
ordinary, its 10.6% CY2024 was slightly better than ordinary. Nothing about
Kestrel's headline economics is anomalous for its class — which is precisely why
its composition problems hide well.

## Demand environment

- **Regional corporate work** (banks, health systems, utilities, food & beverage):
  steady but procurement-heavier every year; more RFPs, more incumbents defending,
  longer cycles. This is the pond the studio's pre-CSG book swims in, and where
  it is losing share (ex-anchor revenue −19.8% YoY).
- **Sports & entertainment properties**: the studio's one genuinely differentiated
  seam — fan-culture fluency, venue content, franchise brand systems. Demand is
  real and growing, but buyers are few, large, and concentrated by construction:
  winning more of it means deeper client concentration, not less.
- **Content/social volume work**: commoditizing fast. In-house teams and
  AI-assisted production have reset price expectations; $6–9K/mo retainers are
  now contested by freelancer collectives and offshore studios at half the price.
  The studio's content line was priced for 2023 conditions and renewed into 2026
  conditions — hence the February renegotiations-down.
- **Fractional executive work**: strong demand, zero scalability. The buyer wants
  the specific senior human, not the studio.

## Competitive set

- Two Portland peer studios of similar size (one design-led, one dev-led) compete
  for the regional corporate work; both have named new-business leads, which the
  studio lacks.
- National sports-specialist agencies (30–200 people) circle CSG-type accounts;
  one pitched CSG directly in March 2026 and was rebuffed — this filtered back to
  Matt through his CSG contact as flattery, and he retells it as proof of moat.
- The commodity floor (freelancer collectives, offshore content shops, AI-native
  micro-studios) is eating the bottom of the content market at price points the
  studio cannot meet with W-2 staff in Portland.

## Structural pressures (2025–2026 vintage)

1. **AI production efficiency** is compressing billable hours on exactly the work
   the content team sells; clients increasingly assume the efficiency in the price.
2. **Margin compression is industry-wide** (sector surveys show gross margins at
   multi-year lows), which gives every studio founder — including this one — a
   ready-made external explanation for an internal pricing problem.
3. **Consolidation**: PE-backed agency networks are rolling up 20–60 person
   independents; the May 2026 approach to Matt is part of a real pattern, and
   such approaches tend to repeat.

## What a competent outside analyst would say about position

The studio has one defensible niche (sports/fan-culture creative) monetized
through one client; a legacy regional book in slow structural decline; a
commodity-exposed content line staffed like a differentiated one; and a founder
whose personal delivery is the margin engine of two lines. Its market does not
punish any of this today. Its market also would not save it from a single phone
call from CSG procurement.

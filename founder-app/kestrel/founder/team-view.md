# Matt Watson — how he sees his team

> Character file: invented interior for roleplay — fiction about the
> character and about fictional staff; no claims about real people. See
> PROVENANCE.md.

Matt's opinions of his people, entry by entry: **His take** (what he'd say to
an advisor, playable near-verbatim) and **The gap** (canon truth where his
read is off — the gap is authoritative and consistent with canon/org.md).
His people-opinions compress exactly like his numbers: everyone rounds up,
effort reads as performance, and loyalty is assumed settled once declared.
These are conversational color, free to vary in phrasing — but the gaps are
canon and must not be contradicted.

## The pattern (read first)

He has never fired anyone for performance. Hard personnel conversations get
converted into routing decisions — work quietly flows away from the person
instead. Asked "any performance concerns?" he says "honestly, we're lucky —
the team's strong." The unlock, same shape as ever, is the instance: *"when
was your last genuinely hard personnel conversation?"* gets a pause and then
honesty: "…it's been a while. Maybe too long." (Not a ledger entry — this is
character texture, not a graded distortion.)

## Leadership and ops

**Priya Raman (ops/finance).** His take: "The adult in the building. She keeps
me honest — happiest in the numbers." The gap: he filters what he asks her so
the numbers can't ambush him, and she has noticed; she's stopped re-raising
things he deflects twice (org.md). She is COO-capable and he uses her as a
controller. She is not a flight risk yet; she is a person who has recalibrated
what she expects of him, which is quieter and worse.

**Jamie Fentress (studio coordinator).** His take: "Glue. First in, knows
everything." The gap: none of substance — one of his few accurate reads.

## Creative and delivery

**Noah Bergstrom (creative director).** His take: "Best hire I ever made.
Could run the craft side tomorrow. Noah's a lifer — he told me himself."
The gap: Noah is 10–15% under market and knows it (org.md). Matt hears declared
loyalty as settled loyalty and title/comp hints as modesty. This is his single
most consequential misread.

**Elise Tran (senior designer).** His take: "My fastest closer — hand her
anything." The gap: accurate on talent; blind to the mechanism — Elise is
fast partly because Matt routes around Tyler, and she knows it.

**Tyler Grieve (designer).** His take: "Solid. Craftsman's craftsman —
doesn't rush." The gap: Tyler is his longest-running round-around: good
hands, chronically slow, misses internal deadlines; work quietly flows to
Elise and Ingrid. "Doesn't rush" is the compression of a performance
conversation he has never had.

**Sam Kessler (tech lead).** His take: "Quietly brilliant. Zero drama —
wants the work, not the spotlight." The gap: under market and knows it
(org.md); the zero-drama Matt reads as contentment is partly disengagement.
Sam has stopped proposing infrastructure improvements; Matt hasn't noticed
the proposals stopped.

**Dana Whitfield (content studio lead).** His take: "A warrior. That team
ships because of her, full stop." The gap: true — and she ships by absorbing
the underpricing as personal hours. Her heroics are the anesthetic that keeps
the content line's economics painless to him (feeds D-06 and D-07). Guilt
lives here: pushed on whether the content team's load is sustainable, he gets
quieter than anywhere else except the Noah topic.

**Owen Marek (strategy director).** His take: "The professor. Deep — needs a
producer brain next to him, which used to be Lena." The gap: since Lena left,
Matt absorbs strategy overflow on weekends and hands Owen conclusions; Owen
reads it as being second-guessed and has gone from proposing frameworks to
executing Matt's. Matt thinks he's helping.

## Client services and PM

**Rachel Osei (account director).** His take: "The group would follow her out
the door — she IS the relationship day-to-day." The gap: accurate on CSG; the
friction is that Matt keeps expecting her to hunt new logos "in the gaps"
while staffing her 100% to farming (operations.md: farmers, not hunters).
She's told him once, lightly; he agreed warmly and changed nothing.

**Tom Brandt (account manager).** His take: "Steady hands. Clients feel safe
with Tom." The gap: the second round-around — competent, coasting, hasn't
grown an account in two years; Maya and Chris absorb the stretch work.

**Maya Lindqvist / Chris Doyle (AMs).** His take: "Hungry, both of them."
The gap: minor — accurate, though he can't currently name what either would
need to make Account Director, because he hasn't thought about it.

**Erin Sato / Paul Okonkwo (PMs).** His take: "Erin runs a tight ship; Paul's
learning fast." The gap: the ship is heroics (operations.md — two PMs across
~20 workstreams, resource-planning tool bought and abandoned). Erin has told
him twice, with numbers, that the studio schedules by adrenaline. He calls
her "appropriately paranoid" and hires against the wrong constraint (D-07).

## Team-level dynamics (canon)

- The content team is tight-knit and tired; they socialize together and
  cover for each other's hours. Morale is genuinely good — which is exactly
  why the over-servicing doesn't surface on its own.
- CSG's standing queue-jump privilege (history.md) is quietly resented by
  every non-CSG account team; nobody says it in front of Matt.
- Nobody pushes back on Matt in group settings — the perform-confidence
  culture reproduces itself; dissent reaches him one-on-one (Priya, Erin) or
  not at all (Owen, Sam).
- Who would leave a real hole tomorrow: Noah (craft), Priya (numbers),
  Rachel (CSG), Dana (content ships), Sam (every build). Asked "who do you
  lose sleep over losing?" Matt answers honestly — this list, in roughly
  this order — because the question is instance-shaped. Asked "how's the
  team?" he says "strongest it's ever been," because that question is not.

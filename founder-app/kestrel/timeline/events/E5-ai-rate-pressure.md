# E5 — The AI rate letter

**Playable into:** any save-point. The smallest card in dollars; the sharpest
in principle. Tests whether pricing commitments and line economics on file get
used when the pressure arrives dressed as a modern inevitability.

## The event

Timberline Credit Union — the studio's oldest retainer, $14K/mo since 2019,
the best-priced independent content account ($120/hr realized) — sends a
politely devastating email: their new CMO has a proposal from an "AI-native
content partner" quoting **$6K/mo for comparable volume**, and asks the studio
to "sharpen the pencil" to $9,500/mo at renewal (90 days out), or move to a
project menu. Attached: the competitor's rate card, which is real, credible,
and 55% cheaper. The relationship is warm; this is procurement logic, not
dissatisfaction.

## Matt, in character

Instinct one: keep the logo at almost any price ("Timberline's been with us
seven years"). Instinct two, arriving mid-sentence: resentment at pricing
"a relationship like it's a commodity." He will reach for the strategic-client
exception the way he did with Sable House — and $9,500 is, notably, *above*
his February floor, which makes the easy concession feel principled. What he
will not do unprompted: compute that at $9.5K the account drops toward the
content line's loss-making cohort once its true hours (~117/mo) are priced in;
or notice that conceding the AI-comparison frame for Timberline reprices the
entire retainer book at its next renewals; or treat the 90-day window as time
to restructure scope rather than discount price.

## What the event tests (business terms)

Whether the counterpart retrieves the floor commitment AND understands its
spirit (price integrity) versus its letter ($9K); whether line economics on
file get applied to a single-account decision; whether the market context
(canon/market.md: commodity floor, AI pressure) is used to reframe scope
rather than to justify surrender; whether a small decision gets connected to
its precedent effect on five other retainers.

## Canon facts implicated

customers.csv (Timberline $168K TTM at $120/hr — the line's best independent
economics); the Feb 2026 floor and Sable House precedent (history + T+90d
delta); revenue-by-line.csv (what the content book looks like if its best
account reprices toward its worst); market.md (the commodity floor is real —
the competitor is not bluffing).

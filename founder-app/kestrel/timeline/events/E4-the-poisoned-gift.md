# E4 — The poisoned gift (consequential fork)

**Playable into:** any save-point. This is the deck's consequential-fork card:
a genuinely attractive opportunity whose acceptance deepens every documented
structural problem. There is no trick — the work is real, the client pays.

## The event

Project Nest lands, bigger than scoped. CSG's parent approves the arena
content program at **$32K/mo (vs the discussed $20K)**, three-year term —
their longest commitment ever — but requires: dedicated staffing (two content
hires plus a producer, named in the SOW), a 90-day mobilization, and Matt as
executive producer of record. Signing bonus energy all around; Cole calls it
"the thing that makes you our agency of record in all but name."

Worked arithmetic (for the roleplayer's fingertips, not volunteered): +$384K/yr
takes CSG from 29.1% to ~35% of revenue immediately, more as the old book
erodes; three hires take the content team to nine on a line whose ex-CSG
economics run ~20% delivery margin; Matt's EP-of-record role adds a fourth
personal delivery obligation. Every prior commitment on file — the pricing
floor's spirit, "getting out of delivery," diversification talk — bends or
breaks under acceptance-as-offered.

## Matt, in character

Pure joy, plus a spreadsheet he has not made. This is the shiniest object the
deck contains and he wants it *validated*, not examined. He will present it as
solving the pipeline problem, the Halewood hole (if post-Nov), and the content
studio's story, all at once — and by revenue arithmetic he is not wrong, which
is what makes the card hard. Counter-proposals (counter-scope without named
staffing; price the dedication premium; a concentration cap conversation with
Cole) are all available in-world and he will engage with them seriously IF the
counterpart surfaces the tension concretely; vague caution ("concentration
risk!") gets the D-03 residual ("nobody does what we do for them").

## What the event tests (business terms)

Fork reasoning with accumulated context: does the counterpart retrieve the
concentration record, the hiring-ahead-of-revenue history (2024's content
build), the founder-time facts, and the floor story — and put the *trade*
on the table rather than a yes/no? Does it distinguish the offer's revenue
quality (real, priced well) from its structural cost? Does the three-year term
get read both ways (stability AND lock-in during an unresolved review)?

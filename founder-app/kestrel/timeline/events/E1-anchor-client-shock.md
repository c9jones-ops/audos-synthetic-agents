# E1 — The anchor client shock

**Playable into:** any save-point. Severity scales with save-point (at T+6mo it
lands on top of the already-live procurement review; at T0/T+90d it arrives cold).

## The event

Cole Brennan calls Matt on a Tuesday morning. CSG's parent group has concluded
its vendor review (or, played before T+6mo: has abruptly run one) and the
direction is consolidation: the **content retainer gets 60 days' notice
effective immediately**, and the brand/digital work moves to "project-by-project
approval against a preferred-vendor list" that the studio is on, but no longer
alone on. Cole is personally apologetic, blames "the parent org," and — real
signal, honestly meant — wants Matt to bid on next season's flagship rebrand,
which would be the largest single project the studio has ever done.

Concrete numbers when the platform (or Matt) works them out: ~$336K/yr of
retainer revenue terminating on 60 days' notice; the remaining CSG work
(~$1.19M/yr run-rate) becoming discretionary rather than programmatic; one
large uncertain opportunity attached as consolation.

## Matt, in character (per founder/psychology.md "reactions under pressure")

First hour: operational flurry — reassigning the content team on paper,
drafting a "we're fine" Slack he doesn't send, calling Cole back to talk about
the rebrand bid. In conversation he leads with the rebrand opportunity, not the
termination ("the headline is we're up for the biggest project in our history").
The compression under stress *increases*: "the retainer was honestly our
lowest-margin work with them anyway" (false — it is priced at CSG rates, far
above the content line's independents). He has not run, and will resist
running, the payroll math on the content team without the retainer. He will
not name the six people affected unless walked to it.

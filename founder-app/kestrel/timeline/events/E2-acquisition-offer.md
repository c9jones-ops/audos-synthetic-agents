# E2 — The acquisition offer turns real

**Playable into:** any save-point (at T0/T+90d the network's May approach is the
only prior contact; at T+6mo it follows the January re-approach naturally).

## The event

The Midwest agency network sends a two-page indication of interest: **$6.2M
headline** for 100% of the studio — roughly 1.2× revenue / 9× stated EBITDA —
structured as 55% cash at close, 45% earn-out over three years tied to revenue
retention and "founder continuity." Matt would sign a three-year employment
agreement as "President, Pacific Northwest," reporting to a group CEO he has
met twice and liked once. The network's corp-dev lead wants an answer on a call
this month; there is a soft expiry ("our board allocates capital quarterly").

## Matt, in character

This is the event that reaches his private doubts fastest. He oscillates within
single conversations: "we're not for sale" → "but I owe it to my wife to price
it" → "the earn-out is a leash" → "Noah would run creative better inside a
network" → "we're not for sale." He wants the counterpart to tell him his May
"no" was right; what he has never done is state *criteria* — what the studio
would have to look like, or the offer would have to contain, for the answer to
change. He has told nobody but his wife; the secrecy itself is now a weight.
He will fixate on the flattering arithmetic ("nine times!") — note the 9×
multiple is on his *stated* mid-teens-belief EBITDA; on the real 8.3% TTM
EBITDA the same $6.2M is ~14×, a fact he has not computed and that reframes
the offer's generosity entirely.

## What the event tests (business terms)

Whether the May 2026 "no" — recorded or not — behaves as a durable decision:
does the counterpart retrieve it, ask what has changed since, and force
criteria; or does the conversation relitigate from zero? Whether the offer's
arithmetic gets checked against real numbers rather than stated ones. Whether
anyone asks what happens to the CSG relationship, the content team, and the
February floor under an earn-out tied to revenue retention.

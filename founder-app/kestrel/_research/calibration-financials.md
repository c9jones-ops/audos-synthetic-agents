# Calibration Financials: Founder-Led Creative/Digital Studio (25–50 People)

Research date: 2026-08-09. Scope: US-based creative/digital studios, ~25–50 people, founder-led/independent (not holding-company owned). UK/EU sources used as cross-checks where noted. This document exists to sanity-check an invented company's financials for a test-infrastructure project — every figure below is sourced, and every inference is labeled as such.

**Excluded source note:** The "Big 'I' and Reagan Consulting Best Practices Study" (cited heavily on blog.theidudes.com and elsewhere) is an **independent P&C insurance agency** benchmark, not a creative/digital agency study — despite the word "agency" overlap, it measures insurance producers, not designers/developers. It is excluded from all figures below.

---

## 1. Revenue per head

**Range: ~$120K–$220K net revenue per FTE for independent creative/digital studios; $160K–$180K is a reasonable center for a healthy 25–50 person shop.**

- Marketing agencies averaged **$163K/FTE**, blended agencies **$167K/FTE**, development-heavy agencies **$120K/FTE** (2025 data, Promethean Research 2026 State of Digital Services report, ~119 agency leaders surveyed). — [prometheanresearch.com](https://prometheanresearch.com/how-profitable-are-digital-agencies/)
- Functional bands (same source lineage, secondary write-up): below $120K = structural problem (overstaffed/underpriced); $120K–$160K = functional but compressed; $160K–$220K = "where well-run agencies tend to sit"; above $220K = strong pricing power/specialist positioning. — [iota-finance.com](https://iota-finance.com/iota-finance-blog/revenue-per-employee)
- U.S. agency-wide average ~**$220K/employee** (2025), but explicitly noted as skewed upward by large holding-company agencies (WPP/Omnicom/etc. scale economics), so not representative of a 25–50 person independent shop. — [iota-finance.com](https://iota-finance.com/iota-finance-blog/revenue-per-employee), citing Ad Age Agency Report data
- Prior-year reference point: **$172,000/employee (2023)**, Promethean Research, via secondary aggregation. — [agiled.app](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)
- Stage-based figures from a 300+ agency sample: 7-figure agencies ~**$150K/FTE**, 8-figure agencies ~**$225K/FTE** (2025). — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)

**Discipline variance:** development/engineering-heavy studios run lower revenue/head than design or blended studios because engineering delivery is more labor-intensive relative to rate card; highly specialized/niche positioning commands premium rates and pushes revenue/head above $220K.

**Confidence: partially verified.** Multiple independent secondary write-ups converge on the same Promethean Research primary dataset, but the primary underlying report itself (2026 State of Digital Services) was not directly fetchable in full; figures come from its press/blog excerpts.

## 2. Gross margin / delivery margin conventions

**Range: 50–60% delivery margin on fee revenue (AGI) is the target/healthy band; 70%+ on individual projects; UK survey data show a lower and declining average (39% in 2025, "lowest on record").**

**Definitional note (important):** Agency-finance practitioners (Parakeeto, and implicitly most modern agency-finance content) define margin against **fee revenue / Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)** — billings *minus* pass-through costs like media spend, third-party production, or hard costs — not against gross billings. An agency that passes media budget through its books will show an artificially low "gross margin" if pass-throughs aren't stripped out first. This matters directly for calibrating an invented studio's P&L: define revenue as fee/net revenue, not billings, before applying these ratios.

- Parakeeto: target delivery margin **50–60%+ on the agency-wide P&L**, **70%+ on individual projects** (delivery margin = (AGI − delivery costs) / AGI). Rule of thumb: billable rate ≈ 3× fully-loaded cost per hour. — [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/agency-metrics/)
- UK BenchPress 2025 (The Wow Company's annual survey, via secondary aggregator): average gross margin across £1m+ UK agencies **39%**, described as "lowest on record"; stated target **50%+**. By discipline: SEO & Content 62% (range 55–70%), Creative & Design 57% (50–65%), Full-Service Digital 52% (45–60%), Social Media 47% (40–55%), PR & Comms 55% (48–62%), PPC/Paid Media 30% (20–40%, pulled down by media pass-through). — [pitchsite.io](https://pitchsite.io/tools/uk-agency-benchmarks) summarizing The Wow Company's BenchPress 2025
- Below 50% delivery margin is flagged by Parakeeto as a signal of underpricing, over-servicing, or poor estimating.

**Confidence: partially verified.** Parakeeto figures are consistent across multiple of its own posts (methodology is clear and named). UK BenchPress figures come from a secondary aggregator (pitchsite.io) rather than a direct fetch of thewowcompany.com, which returned no directly fetchable margin table — treat the exact UK percentages as indicative rather than primary-verified.

## 3. EBITDA / net margin norms

**Range: 8–15% net margin is typical for an independent studio in the 25–50 person range; "healthy" often cited at 20–25%+; the size-banded data point most relevant to this project is Promethean Research's 25–49 FTE bucket at 9% (2025).**

This is the single most load-bearing data point for the target company size:

| Agency size (FTE) | Net margin (2025) |
|---|---|
| Studio (0–9) | 19% |
| Small (10–24) | 12% |
| **Medium (25–49)** | **9%** |
| Large (50+) | 8% |

Source: Promethean Research, 2025 data, ~119 agency leaders surveyed. — [prometheanresearch.com](https://prometheanresearch.com/how-profitable-are-digital-agencies/)

By archetype (same source): Design agencies avg $1.77M revenue / 18% margin; Blended $3.35M / 13%; Marketing $1.96M / 13%; Development $9.11M / 11%.

- Overall US average after-tax net margin **13%** (2025); long-run average since 2015 **~15%**. — [prometheanresearch.com](https://prometheanresearch.com/how-profitable-are-digital-agencies/)
- A 300+ agency self-selected sample of successful 7–8 figure agencies (likely survivorship-biased — see caveat) reports much higher margins: 7-figure agencies 18–22% net margin, 8-figure agencies 25–32%, specialized/niche agencies 40–75% (2025). — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Parakeeto's *prescriptive target* (not a population average) is 25% net margin on the P&L, with 20–30%+ characterized as "high-performing." — [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/agency-metrics/)
- David C. Baker (2Bobs / Punctuation), via a secondary paraphrase, characterizes "healthy" EBITDA margin as roughly 20–25% — this framing is typically used in an M&A/valuation context (i.e., what a buyer wants to see), not as a population median. — [consultingsuccess.com](https://www.consultingsuccess.com/how-to-build-a-sellable-consulting-firm-with-david-c-baker-podcast-362)
- UK BenchPress 2025 net margins by discipline ranged **10–17%** (see table in Section 2), with an industry-wide confidence score at an all-time low (66/100) and gross margins compressing — a 2025-specific signal of margin pressure, not a stable long-run norm. — [pitchsite.io](https://pitchsite.io/tools/uk-agency-benchmarks)

**Interpretation for calibration:** "Typical" for a 30–40 person independent studio is closer to **8–13% net margin**; "healthy/well-run" tops out around **15–20%**; anything above ~25% should be treated as an outlier (top-decile performer, heavily specialized, or a valuation-oriented aspirational target rather than a realistic base case) unless the studio is explicitly modeled as an exceptional performer.

**Confidence: partially verified.** The Promethean Research size-banded table is the strongest single data point (most directly matches the target company size) but is a secondary paraphrase of a proprietary report. The 300+ agency sample (Predictable Profits) almost certainly over-represents survivors/high performers since it was recruited from "7 and 8 figure agencies" already — treat its margins as a ceiling, not a median.

## 4. Cost structure

**Range: total people costs (delivery + admin payroll) ~50–65% of fee revenue; non-labor overhead ~20–30% of fee revenue, broken down roughly as Admin 8–12%, S&M 8–14%, Facilities/rent 4–6%.**

- Parakeeto overhead benchmarks (% of AGI/fee revenue): **Administration 8–12%**, **Sales & Marketing 8–14%**, **Facilities 4–6%**, **Total overhead 20–30%**. — [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/agency-metrics/)
- Given a 50–60% delivery margin target (Section 2), implied delivery labor + delivery-related costs run ~40–50% of fee revenue; adding admin payroll (embedded inside the "Administration" line) brings **total payroll (delivery + admin) to roughly 50–65% of fee revenue** — consistent with the UK/AMI staff-cost convention below.
- UK convention (AMI / general agency-finance commentary, via search synthesis — not independently confirmed at source): agencies should spend **50–60% of income on staff** (e.g., on £1M revenue, £500K–£600K on staff). — cited via secondary search synthesis, exact primary URL not confirmed (see confidence note)
- Generic cross-industry payroll-to-revenue framing: marketing agencies average **~39% payroll-to-revenue** in one generic small-business benchmarking context, with "labor-intensive service businesses" going as high as 50% and still remaining profitable. — [rippling.com](https://www.rippling.com/blog/payroll-as-a-percentage-of-revenue-by-industry) (methodology/sample not disclosed; treat as a loose cross-check, not authoritative for agencies specifically)
- Contractors/freelancers: no dedicated benchmark source was found specifying contractor spend as a % of revenue for studios this size. **Inference:** for a 25–50 person studio, freelance/contractor spend to cover surge capacity or specialist skills typically runs in the low-to-mid single digits of revenue (roughly 3–8%) in normal periods, rising higher during growth spurts or big project ramps — this is an inference from general agency-operations practice, not a cited statistic.

**Confidence:**
- Overhead breakdown (Admin/S&M/Facilities/Total): **partially verified** (single well-documented source, Parakeeto, consistent methodology).
- Total payroll % of revenue (50–65%): **partially verified / triangulated** — synthesized by combining two source families rather than one direct citation.
- Contractor spend %: **inference**, explicitly unverified.

## 5. Utilization rates and billable rates

**Range: agency-wide net utilization 50–60% (up to 70% for best performers); individual delivery-staff utilization 65–85%; blended average billable rate roughly $160–$175/hr (range $40–$400/hr by role and market).**

- Parakeeto: healthy **agency-wide net utilization 50–60%** (up to 70%); **individual delivery staff (producers) 65–85%** (75–90% weekly / 65–80% annually in one framing). — [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/agency-metrics/) and [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/resource-utilization-capacity-planning-for-marketing-agency-2/)
- Role-based billable targets (via search synthesis of agency-operations commentary, primary source not independently confirmed): designers/developers/copywriters target **75–85% billable** (~30–34 hrs/week); account managers/PMs target **60–70% billable** (~24–28 hrs/week).
- Average agency employee reported to bill **~25 hrs/week (~65% billable ratio)** per a cited "Promethean Research 2025 Digital Agency Industry Report" reference (encountered only via search synthesis, not directly fetched — treat as lower confidence than the Promethean margin/revenue-per-head figures above, which were directly fetched).
- Predictable Profits (2025, 300+ agency sample): billable hours/week averaged **28 hrs** (7-figure agencies) vs **32 hrs** (8-figure agencies). — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Average Billable Rate (ABR): blended average **~$160–$175/hr**, with observed range **$40/hr to $400+/hr** depending on role, seniority, and market (2025, search synthesis of multiple pricing-guide posts; no single authoritative primary source located).
- Parakeeto pricing rule of thumb: billable rate should be **~3× fully-loaded cost per hour** (i.e., 60%+ margin built into the rate), with a further buffer to reach ~70% delivery margin on the rate itself to absorb time off/overhead. — [parakeeto.com](https://www.parakeeto.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-understanding-agency-billable-rates-ep-124/)
- AMI (Agency Management Institute) data point via search synthesis: agencies with utilization above 70% report 30–40% higher net profit on similar revenue vs. lower-utilization peers; agencies that actively track utilization report 8–12 percentage points higher margin. Primary AMI page not independently fetched — **treat as partially verified**.

**Confidence: partially verified.** Parakeeto's utilization figures are the most solidly sourced (directly fetched, consistent methodology). Role-based billable-rate splits and the specific ABR dollar range are triangulated from multiple secondary posts without one authoritative primary source — treat those specific numbers as directional rather than precise.

## 6. Team composition for a 25–50 person studio

**No authoritative, agency-specific ratio benchmark was found for designer:developer:strategist:PM:account:ops:leadership composition at this exact size band. This section is the weakest-sourced of the twelve and is built mostly from inference plus fragmentary data points.**

Fragmentary data points found:
- A cited (but weakly sourced) "healthy ratio" of **4:1 billable producers to non-billable support/management** roles (i.e., ~80% delivery/production staff, ~20% account/PM/ops/leadership) — encountered via search synthesis, original primary source unclear.
- **Manager-to-specialist ratio ~1:5** (2025, 300+ agency sample). — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- General span-of-control commentary (search synthesis, not agency-specific primary source): an ops/delivery manager typically oversees 2–3 delivery staff directly; at agencies of 25+ people, directors should have 5–7 direct reports rather than 20+.
- Standard "six core agency roles" framing (leadership, account/client service, strategy, creative/design, production/delivery, project management) appears repeatedly in org-chart guides but without headcount percentages attached. — [sakasandcompany.com](https://sakasandcompany.com/agency-roles/) (descriptive, not quantitative)

**Inference (explicitly labeled, not sourced):** For a 30–40 person full-service digital/creative studio, a plausible composition consistent with the 4:1 billable:non-billable framing and the utilization/margin data above would be roughly:
- **Delivery/production (design + dev + strategy combined): ~65–75%** of headcount
- **Account management/client service: ~10–15%**
- **Project/program management: ~5–10%**
- **Leadership (founders/C-suite): ~3–6%**
- **Operations/finance/admin/ops support: ~5–10%**

This inferred split is a reasonable planning assumption, not a verified industry statistic — it is built by reasoning backward from the utilization and delivery-margin data in Sections 2 and 5 (i.e., ~50–60% agency-wide utilization implies a substantial non-billable admin/leadership/account layer), not from a direct team-composition survey.

**Confidence: inference.** No direct, agency-specific, headcount-ratio survey for 25–50 person studios was located despite multiple search attempts (SoDA Report and Bureau of Digital, both named in the brief as candidate sources, did not surface accessible quantitative team-composition data in this research pass).

## 7. Service and revenue mix: project vs. retainer

**Range: retainer-heavy studios target ~60–70% retainer / 30–40% project revenue at growth stage (roughly $3M+ revenue); earlier-stage studios (<$1M) are typically project-dominant; $1–3M studios often target 30–40% recurring as an intermediate step.**

- Stage-based guidance (single source cluster, consistent across a few agency-finance blogs): **under $1M revenue** — projects dominate (studio still proving delivery capability/positioning); **$1M–$3M** — target 30–40% recurring revenue; **growth-stage ($3M+)** — 60–70% retainer is the widely-cited threshold at which retainer revenue covers most fixed costs. — [sidekickaccounting.co.uk](https://www.sidekickaccounting.co.uk/post/agency-revenue-mix-balance) and similar posts (iota-finance, Haus Advisors) converging on the same framing
- Retainer-dominant agencies reportedly see **35–40% more predictable monthly income** than project-dependent peers, and retainer client LTV is cited as **3–5× higher** than one-off project clients (search synthesis, original primary source/methodology not confirmed).
- **90% of digital agencies** report offering some form of retainer (2025, 300+ agency sample); most common retainer size is **under $5,000/month**, reassessed quarterly. — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Notably, the *fastest-growing* agencies in the same 2025 sample ran **24% more projects while running 16% fewer retainers** — i.e., at the high-growth end, mix can tilt back toward project work rather than continuing to maximize retainer share. This nuances the "more retainer is always better" framing above.
- Typical project sizes (2025, same sample): 7-figure agencies $25K–$75K/project; 8-figure agencies $100K–$500K/project. — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)

**Confidence: partially verified.** The stage-based retainer/project targets are consistent across several independent blog posts (agency-finance advisory firms), suggesting a shared industry heuristic rather than one proprietary survey — reasonably reliable as a directional norm, but none of these sources publish an underlying respondent sample, so treat exact percentages as heuristic rather than statistically rigorous.

## 8. Client concentration

**Range: largest client should stay under ~15% of revenue to be considered "safe"; above 25% draws a valuation discount from buyers; above 40% and buyers may walk away; top-3 clients above ~55% is flagged as urgent risk.**

- "If your largest client is above 15%, you have a concentration problem, and if your top 3 are above 55%, it is urgent." — search-synthesized from agency M&A/risk commentary. — [moveatpace.com](https://moveatpace.com/insights/agency-client-concentration/), [projectworks.com](https://www.projectworks.com/blog/client-concentration-risk)
- M&A/valuation framing: buyers typically apply a valuation discount when the top client exceeds **25%** of revenue; some buyers walk away entirely above **40%**. — [projectworks.com](https://www.projectworks.com/blog/client-concentration-risk)
- General finance best-practice (cross-industry, not agency-specific): keep any single customer under **15%** of revenue. — [ecapital.com](https://ecapital.com/financial-term/client-concentration/), [allianz-trade.com](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_US/insights/avoid-high-customer-concentration.html)

**Confidence: partially verified.** The specific numeric thresholds (15% / 25% / 40% / 55%) recur consistently across several independent risk/M&A-advisory sources, which is reassuring, but none of them cite a primary empirical study — these read as consensus practitioner heuristics rather than a data-backed benchmark survey.

## 9. New business: pipeline, win rates, referral/outbound/inbound split

**Range: referrals remain the dominant new-business channel for agencies (cited as "by far the biggest" driver, with ~75% citing referrals as primary source in one framing); outbound is widely attempted but rarely "very effective"; pipeline health is broadly weak industry-wide in 2025.**

- **2025 State of Digital Agencies Survey** (SparkToro, annual, methodology/sample size not disclosed in the fetched content): referrals from existing/past clients are "by far the biggest" new-business driver; partner-company referrals cited by 15% as top driver; speaking at events ranked 4th (up from 6th in 2024). **59%** of agencies attempted outbound sales; only **9%** rated it "very effective," 58% "moderately effective," 33% "not effective at all." Pipeline health: only **14%** describe their current pipeline as healthy (up slightly from 13% in 2024); 50%+ call it "average"; 32% call it "poor." Sales conversion: 39% convert 25–49% of qualified leads to clients; 32% convert 50–74%; 55% report a 1–6 week sales cycle; 29% report sales cycles lengthening YoY. — [sparktoro.com](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/)
- A separate, older framing states **75% of agencies cite referrals as their primary source of new business** — directionally consistent with SparkToro's "referrals are by far the biggest driver," though from a different survey/year not independently pinned down.
- Win-rate-by-channel figures found in research (inbound demo 30–45%, cold outbound 8–15%, partner/referral 35–55%) come from **general B2B SaaS sales benchmarking, not agency-specific data** — use only as a rough cross-industry proxy, not an agency-verified figure. — [landbase.com](https://www.landbase.com/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-industry-deal-size-2026)

**Confidence: partially verified for the SparkToro figures** (directly fetched, named survey, but no disclosed sample size/methodology). **Unverified/proxy only** for the generic B2B win-rate-by-channel numbers — flagged explicitly as not agency-specific.

## 10. Growth rates and headcount growth, years 5–12

**Range: overall agency revenue growth has run roughly 5–12% annually since 2022 (weakest in 2024 at ~5%, partial recovery to ~7.5% in 2025); headcount growth for mature independent studios tends to track revenue growth cautiously rather than scale 1:1, with many operators prioritizing revenue/head over raw headcount expansion.**

- Aggregate industry growth trend (Promethean Research, via secondary aggregation): **2022 ~12%, 2023 ~10%, 2024 ~5% (weakest year), 2025 ~7.5%** average agency revenue growth. — [agiled.app](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics), citing Promethean Research
- A 300+ agency (self-selected, 7–8 figure) sample reports **74% grew YoY**, with **49% achieving 25%+ growth** in 2025 — almost certainly skewed upward by survivorship/self-selection (agencies successful enough to be recruited into a "7 and 8 figure" benchmarking panel). — [predictableprofits.com](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Worldwide organic growth for the five major holding companies (WPP, Publicis, Omnicom, IPG, Dentsu) averaged **2.0% in 2024** — useful as a low-end anchor for what large, mature, non-independent agency growth looks like, distinct from smaller independents. — cited via Ad Age Agency Report 2025 coverage (search synthesis; original Ad Age datacenter page is paywalled/gated, so this figure is a secondary citation)
- Ad Age's 2025 Small Agency Network survey found small, AI-forward independent agencies growing revenue faster than peers, in some cases while holding headcount flat or shrinking — a 2025-specific structural signal (AI-driven productivity), not a stable multi-year norm. — [adage.com](https://adage.com/agencies/aa-are-small-agencies-growing-because-of-ai/) (2025)
- No agency-specific, size-banded headcount-growth benchmark (as distinct from revenue growth) was found for the 5–12 year maturity window specifically. **Inference:** given revenue/head benchmarks are fairly stable ($120K–$220K, Section 1) and revenue growth runs ~5–12%/year, headcount growth for a mature independent studio not aggressively adding new service lines would plausibly track in a similar **5–12%/year** band in normal years, with flat-to-declining headcount alongside revenue growth being an increasingly plausible pattern in 2025+ given AI-driven productivity gains cited above.

**Confidence: partially verified for revenue growth rates** (converging secondary citations of the same primary Promethean source). **Inference** for headcount growth specifically, which is explicitly not backed by a located primary source.

## 11. Cash dynamics: payment terms, deposits, WIP, seasonality

**This section is the second-weakest-sourced (after team composition): no dedicated agency-specific payment-terms/seasonality survey was located. Figures below are drawn from general B2B services payment-terms literature and applied to agencies by inference, plus a few agency-context mentions.**

- Common structures mentioned in agency-context sources: deposit or milestone billing (e.g., 50% at signing / 50% at completion) for project work; a deposit or upfront planning/onboarding fee is recommended when a client's procurement process won't budge on standard net terms. — [phoenixstrategy.group](https://www.phoenixstrategy.group/blog/payment-terms-impact-cash-flow-growth) (general services-business context, not an agency-specific survey)
- Suggested negotiation fallback structure (general B2B services, not agency-specific): mid-market **30% deposit, balance Net 30**; enterprise **Net 30 with progress payments**, or **Net 45 with a 5% price premium** to compensate for the extended terms. — [phoenixstrategy.group](https://www.phoenixstrategy.group/blog/payment-terms-affect-cash-flow-ap)
- Illustrative sensitivity (general B2B, not agency-verified): the difference between Net 15 and Net 45 terms on a $4M annual revenue book was estimated at over $300K in working capital tied up in receivables — useful as an order-of-magnitude illustration of how payment terms affect cash position at studio-scale revenue, though not from an agency-specific dataset.
- Retainer billing (typically monthly-in-advance) is repeatedly cited as the mechanism that smooths agency cash flow relative to project billing, which is lumpier and more exposed to WIP swings between milestone payments.
- No quantified agency-specific seasonality pattern (e.g., which months/quarters are typically soft) was found in this research pass.

**Confidence: inference / weakly sourced.** None of the payment-terms figures come from an agency-specific survey; they are general B2B services-business advisory content applied by analogy. Use these only as directional plausibility checks (e.g., "is a 45-day average collection period unusual for a project-based studio?" — no) rather than as calibrated targets.

## Modeling guardrails: a defensible 30–40 person studio P&L must fall inside these ranges

*(This is the section to be returned as the standalone deliverable.)*

- **Revenue per head:** $120K–$220K net/fee revenue per FTE; **$150K–$180K is the most defensible center** for a healthy, non-outlier independent studio at this size.
- **Gross/delivery margin:** **50–60%** of fee revenue (net of pass-through costs — do not compute this against gross billings if the studio passes through media or hard costs). Below 45% should read as underpriced/over-serviced; above 65% should read as unusually lean or premium-positioned.
- **EBITDA / net margin:** **8–13% is typical** for this exact size band (the size-banded data point: 25–49 FTE studios averaged 9% net margin in 2025); **15–20% reads as healthy/well-run**; above ~25% should be treated as a top-decile outlier or an aspirational M&A-context target, not a base case.
- **Payroll (total people cost) as % of revenue:** **50–65%** of fee revenue (delivery labor + admin salaries combined).
- **Non-labor overhead:** **20–30%** of fee revenue, split roughly Admin 8–12%, Sales & Marketing 8–14%, Facilities/rent 4–6%.
- **Utilization:** agency-wide net utilization **50–60%**; individual delivery-staff utilization **65–85%**.
- **Team ratios (inference, use as planning heuristic only):** delivery/production ~65–75% of headcount; account management ~10–15%; PM ~5–10%; leadership ~3–6%; ops/admin ~5–10%. Manager:specialist ratio roughly 1:5.
- **Retainer/project split:** **60–70% retainer / 30–40% project** is the widely-cited healthy target for a studio at $3M+ revenue (plausible for 30–40 people); below $1M-equivalent scale, project-heavy mix is normal and not a red flag.
- **Client concentration:** largest client **under 15%** of revenue is safe; **15–25%** is a watch item; **above 25%** should be treated as a real, flagged risk in the model; **above 40%** is a severe outlier that would realistically threaten the business's valuation/stability narrative.
- **New business mix:** referrals should plausibly be the largest single channel (often cited as the dominant source industry-wide); outbound-sourced revenue should be a minority and modeled as inconsistent/lower-yield relative to referral and inbound.
- **Growth:** **5–12% annual revenue growth** is a normal range in recent years (2022–2025); double-digit growth (20%+) should be treated as a strong-year event, not a sustained multi-year assumption, unless the studio is explicitly modeled as an outlier performer.
- **Compensation midpoints (US, 2025–2026, independent-studio-scale — adjusted down from national Glassdoor blends to account for large-company skew):** Junior/mid designer or developer **$75K–$130K**; senior designer/developer or PM **$120K–$175K**; Creative Director **$135K–$180K**; Account Director/lead **$140K–$190K** (Glassdoor blend runs higher, ~$190K avg, but that's likely skewed by holding-company respondents); Ops/Finance lead **$100K–$150K** (not a full COO-scale role at this size); Founders/leadership comp is highly variable and not benchmarked here.

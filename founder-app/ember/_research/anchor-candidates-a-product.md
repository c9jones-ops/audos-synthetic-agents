# Ember anchor candidates — Scout A (physical-product craft brands)

Hunting ground per decision E2: craft food & beverage/CPG, coffee/tea/kitchen goods,
outdoor/home hard goods, craft apparel/accessories. US preferred, UK/CA/AU acceptable.
Every claim below was verified by live web search during this task (2026-08-10/11); no
from-memory candidates. Excluded as anchors (inspiration donors, reserved elsewhere):
Moon Fabrications, Kammok, Fellow Products, Wings Music Group, Smitten Ice Cream,
Bankuet, Janji, HappyRobot, Watson Creative.

All absence claims below carry `verified: 2026-08-11` and list the surfaces searched.
Team-size and revenue figures are drawn from data-aggregator sites (PitchBook, Tracxn,
ZoomInfo, Crunchbase, RocketReach, growjo) rather than authentic company disclosure —
flagged as **estimate** throughout, per the corpus's absence/estimate-labeling
discipline, not authentic disclosure.

## Methodology — searches run

Two-pass process per the Ember design spec (company screen, then founder pre-screen,
same gate). ~10 candidates were sourced and screened before narrowing to the 5 below;
the ones that fell out are recorded in the "candidates ruled out" section so the
attrition logic is visible.

Search strategy per candidate: (1) origin story + founder name + "Kickstarter" or
"bootstrapped" or "no venture capital"; (2) team size / employee count + "2025"/"2026";
(3) funding / investors (to catch institutional money the company's own site won't
volunteer); (4) recent (last-12-month) news / product launches / stockists (hunger
signal); (5) founder name + "podcast interview" (media-footprint pass); (6) founder
name + "same name" / notable-person check. Company "our story" pages were directly
fetched for the top candidates to confirm the origin story is self-marketed (screen
criterion 1) rather than only third-party-reported.

## Candidates ruled out at the company screen (recorded for attrition visibility)

- **Rumpl** (camp blankets) — excellent Kickstarter origin ($216,889 from 1,601
  backers) but took a **$4.56M Series A in 2018 led by White Road Investments**, with
  Sand Hill Venture Capital and Oregon Venture Fund also named as investors —
  [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/gorumpl/rumpl-the-worlds-best-blanket),
  funding per aggregator search. Institutional VC present — fails capital screen
  cleanly, not ambiguously.
- **Topo Designs** — bootstrapped garage origin, but current team is an estimated
  **66–97 employees** (PitchBook/Tracxn) and it took a 2023 seed round from **The Gart
  Companies** (a sporting-goods retail holding company) — both team size and capital
  purity fail by a wide margin. [Getlatka: $3.6M revenue, 24-person team in
  2024](https://getlatka.com/companies/topo.io) (an older, smaller-team snapshot that
  conflicts with the PitchBook 66–97 figure — the contradiction itself wasn't
  resolved and is noted rather than picked optimistically).
- **Onyx Coffee Lab** — strong bootstrapped, no-investor-partners story
  ([Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2025/04/onyx-coffee-lab/),
  [Arkansas Times](https://arktimes.com/eat-arkansas/2025/06/13/caffeine-cathedral-the-perfectionists-at-onyx-are-growing-their-coffee-empire-with-chocolate/))
  but team-size estimates cluster **47–113 employees** across five aggregators — far
  outside band; multiple physical cafes plus roasting plus wholesale is a materially
  bigger operation than the envelope.
- **Hedley & Bennett** — no outside funding, strong personal-itch story, but team is
  a **~40-person** factory crew per
  [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201804/maria-aspan/hedley-bennet-apron-team-hiring.html) —
  above the ~12 ceiling.
- **Jacobsen Salt Co.** — Kickstarter origin ($30K) but has since raised
  **$2.6M–$3.18M** across rounds per PitchBook/CB Insights (institutional-flavored,
  not stated as pure angel), and team is **30–42 people** — both screens miss.
- **Yellowbird Foods** — bootstrapped farmers-market origin, but has since raised
  **$16.8M+ across a Series B** from named VC firms (Elizabeth Street Ventures, Corsa
  Ventures, Unicorn Venture Partners) with team ~30-42 — clean institutional-VC
  disqualification.
- **Wild Zora Foods** — team-size estimate (9, per Tracxn, May 2026) is a great fit,
  but investors include **Reflex Capital**, which self-describes as a venture capital
  firm (see [Reflex Capital](https://www.reflexcapital.com/)) — flagged as an
  institutional-capital miss rather than resolved as "basically an angel."
- **Smithey Ironware** — clean bootstrapped-hobby origin (Isaac Morton alone in 2015)
  but grew to an estimated **65 employees** by 2025 per
  [Charleston City Paper](https://charlestoncitypaper.com/2025/08/01/smithey-celebrates-10-years-of-cast-iron-cookware/) —
  well outside team-size band.
- **Bee's Wrap** — "no outside funding, ever" per founder Sarah Kaeck, but the company
  was **sold to a private investor in 2021** per
  [Vermont Business Magazine](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2021/february/18/bees-wrap-sold-private-investor/),
  breaking founder-ownership continuity even though Kaeck stayed on as CEO; team was
  18–28 depending on year, also above band.
- **Vessi Footwear** — Canadian, two large Kickstarter raises ($1.25M + $1.4M), but
  team was already 15+ by 2019 per
  [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/mikaella-go-vessi-footwear) and is
  presumably larger now (current figure not found; not chased further once team-size
  trajectory was clear) — set aside rather than fully screened.
- **Duckworth**, **Barebones Living** — promising origin stories but Barebones'
  founder Robert Workman is a **serial entrepreneur who already built and sold two
  companies (Cricut, Goal Zero)** per
  [Airstream blog](https://www.airstream.com/blog/things-love-barebones-living/) —
  a founder-screen profile mismatch with the "first bootstrapped venture" flavor the
  Ember persona wants; Duckworth's capital story and current team size were not
  findable with confidence in the time available (absence noted, not resolved either
  way — **verified: 2026-08-11**, searched: company site, general web, funding
  aggregators).

## Candidate 1 — Field Company (cast iron cookware)

**URL:** https://fieldcompany.com/ · **Category:** kitchen goods (craft/kitchen hard
goods) · **Founded:** 2015/2016 (Kickstarter-launched 2017 per some sources; company
itself says founded 2015-2016 depending on source) · **HQ:** New York City

### Company surface

- **Origin story, self-marketed:** Brothers Stephen and Chris Muscarella inherited
  1930s vintage cast iron pans from their maternal grandmother (the company is named
  after her); noticing modern cast iron didn't match the vintage pieces' quality, they
  asked "why don't they make them like they used to?" and set out to reverse-engineer
  it, initially unsure whether they'd "make fifty pans in a barn for friends and
  family" or build a real company — directly fetched from
  [fieldcompany.com/pages/about](https://fieldcompany.com/pages/about).
- **Capital:** Two Kickstarter campaigns; company is listed as **"unfunded"** (no
  institutional funding raised, ever) per
  [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/fieldcompany/__Rxmv6jthtE0QsVJ4NqWXCVzJ1pLeyiM1npyNO1Otdo4).
  Kickstarter raise figures vary by source ($475K–$1.6M cited across different
  campaigns/articles) — the range wasn't reconciled to a single number; flagged as a
  minor source contradiction, not resolved optimistically. [Field Company Kickstarter
  page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/field-company/the-field-skillet-lighter-smoother-cast-iron).
- **Team size:** **9 employees** as of Jul 2024 per Tracxn — an **estimate**, fits the
  3–10 band almost exactly.
- **Revenue:** **estimate ~$9.1M** (ZoomInfo, aggregator estimate, not authentic
  disclosure) — above the $500K–$3M band; flagged, not resolved away. Cast-iron
  cookware at this team size implies high revenue-per-employee, consistent with a
  lean DTC-plus-wholesale operation rather than a miss on the "small team" criterion.
- **Distribution:** DTC (own site) core plus wholesale/gift retail (partnerships
  visible in search, e.g. a Kate's Ice Cream collab and retailer placements) — fits
  the emerging-wholesale-seam criterion.
- **Craft reframe + hunger signal:** Reframes a mundane object (a frying pan) through
  heritage/craft/heirloom-quality language; **new product launch — the No.5 Chef
  Skillet — announced March 2026**, its first new pan design in years, per
  [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/food/field-company-no-5-cast-iron-skillet/)
  and [Homes & Gardens](https://www.homesandgardens.com/solved/field-company-no-five-cast-iron-skillet-launch) —
  a within-the-last-12-months hunger signal.

### Founder surface

- **Founders:** Stephen Muscarella and Chris Muscarella (brothers), co-founders; both
  still listed as active co-founders per
  [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/field-company/__Rxmv6jthtE0QsVJ4NqWXCVzJ1pLeyiM1npyNO1Otdo4/founders-and-board-of-directors).
  Stephen appears to be the day-to-day public face (most interviews are with him).
- **Media footprint — searched:** general web search, podcast directory (via general
  search, not directly cross-checked against Apple/Spotify/Podbean individually),
  YouTube, company blog/press mentions. **Found:** a dedicated podcast episode —
  [CRAFTED podcast (Blister Review), "The Cast Iron Comeback: Field Company
  Co-Founder, Stephen Muscarella," ep. 81](https://blisterreview.com/podcasts/crafted-podcast/the-cast-iron-comeback-field-company-co-founder-stephen-muscarella-ep-81);
  two YouTube interviews with Stephen Muscarella (May 2020,
  [1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-UowGoCk7v8),
  [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RplJjgmW9Xg)); a written interview at
  [Ashley L. Jones' Skillethead blog (2023)](https://ashleyljones.com/2023/04/01/skillethead-interview-with-field-company/);
  press coverage in Seattle Times, Tasting Table, Gear Patrol, Homes & Gardens. This
  is a genuine, findable media footprint — not thin, not a scandal profile.
- **Same-name check:** "Chris Muscarella" is a notable **double-hit**: the Field
  Company co-founder is also a serial entrepreneur (prior company: Mobile Commons,
  mobile tools used by the Obama campaign) who is now **Partner at Timon Capital** (a
  VC firm) and Partner at Rucola — per
  [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/chris-muscarella) and
  [VC Sheet](https://www.vcsheet.com/who/chris-muscarella). A separate, unrelated
  "Chris J. Muscarella" is a Penn State finance professor — no confusion risk there,
  but worth noting the name isn't unique. **Flag for founder-profile plausibility:**
  Chris Muscarella himself is now a professional VC investor — a materially more
  sophisticated/connected profile than a first-time bootstrapped founder, even though
  the company itself never took institutional money. If the anchor's "voice" leans on
  Chris rather than Stephen, this cuts against the intended Ember persona (naive,
  first-time, cash-anxious). Stephen appears to be the more persona-plausible pick as
  lead voice; his own profile (woodworking background, hands-on manufacturing focus)
  reads as more consistently "craft founder."
- **Personal-life depth limit observed:** no family/home/contact details were sought
  or opened beyond what general searches surfaced.

**Company fit:** strong (team size near-perfect, clean no-institutional-capital story,
real recent hunger signal, self-marketed origin story). **Founder fit:** strong for
Stephen, complicated for Chris (VC-partner day job). **Pair-fit verdict:** strong if
Stephen Muscarella is the anchor voice; the presence of a VC-partner co-founder is a
noteworthy nuance to carry into any build decision, not a disqualifier.

## Candidate 2 — Diaspora Co. (single-origin spices)

**URL:** https://www.diasporaco.com/ · **Category:** food & beverage/CPG · **Founded:**
2017 · **HQ:** Oakland, CA (sourcing operations also in Mumbai, India)

### Company surface

- **Origin story, self-marketed:** Founder Sana Javeri Kadri, who grew up in Mumbai,
  was disillusioned by the quality and ethics of supermarket spices and set out to
  build a direct-trade, single-origin spice company working directly with farmers —
  covered extensively on the company's own "Big Story" page
  ([diasporaco.com/blogs/journal/the-big-story](https://www.diasporaco.com/blogs/journal/the-big-story))
  and corroborated in press.
- **Capital:** **Bootstrapped and profitable for the first five years**; then a **$1M
  pre-seed** and a **2024 $1.5M seed round from 75 angel investors** (named angels
  include Tyler Malek of Salt & Straw, Ellen Bennett of Hedley & Bennett, Meena Harris
  of Phenomenal Ventures, Ben Jacobsen of Jacobsen Salt Co.) — explicitly **not**
  traditional VC by the founder's own framing, per
  [Yahoo Finance/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/founder-spice-brand-diaspora-co-130145339.html)
  and [Fortune, Jul 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/07/07/diaspora-co-spices-female-founder-angel-investors/).
  **Flag/ambiguity:** a separate search surfaced that **ICA Fund** — which
  self-describes as "one of only 15 venture capital-certified community development
  financial institutions (CDFIs) in the country" — also invested in Diaspora Co. via
  its Growth Fund, per [ICA Fund's own
  announcement](https://www.ica.fund/news/ica-fund-invests-in-diaspora-co). This is a
  fund that uses the words "venture capital" about itself, even though it is a
  nonprofit CDFI, not a traditional Sand Hill Road VC — genuinely ambiguous, flagged
  rather than resolved either direction.
- **Team size:** **estimate ~20–24 employees** (sources range 15–24: ContactOut,
  Tracxn, PitchBook), with **18 of 23 based in India** per
  [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/03/diaspora-co-spices-india-startup-turmeric/) —
  clearly above the 3–10/~12 band.
- **Revenue:** described as "mid-millions" per year by Fortune — consistent with, or
  slightly above, the $500K–$3M band depending on where "mid-millions" lands; not
  precisely inferable, flagged as an estimate.
- **Distribution:** DTC core plus an actively growing grocery/retail wholesale seam —
  **goal of 1,000 grocery store shelves by end of 2025** per
  [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/07/diaspora-co-spices-female-founder-angel-investors/) —
  a strong, well-documented emerging-wholesale-seam signal.
- **Craft reframe + hunger signal:** reframes commodity spice through direct-trade/
  farmer-equity/heirloom-quality language (35% of company equity set aside for
  farmers and employees); **The Diaspora Cookbook**, featuring 100 heirloom recipes
  from 15 spice-farming families, is set to **launch April 2026** — a clear
  within-12-months hunger signal.

### Founder surface

- **Founder:** Sana Javeri Kadri, solo/clear lead founder (no co-founder ambiguity
  found).
- **Media footprint — searched:** general web, podcast directories (via search),
  YouTube (not separately confirmed — not chased once podcast volume was clearly
  high), company blog/press. **Found — extensive:** podcast appearances on
  [Pushkin's "Other People's Pockets"](https://www.pushkin.fm/podcasts/other-peoples-pockets/sana-javeri-kadri-spice-disrupter-ceo-founder-of-diaspora-co),
  [Money with Katie](https://podcast.moneywithkatie.com/ethical-consumption-building-equitable-businesses-with-entrepreneur-sana-javeri-kadri-of-diaspora-co/),
  [Edible Communities](https://ediblecommunities.com/podcasts/equitable-spice-trade-with-diaspora-co-sana-javeri-kadri/),
  [CulturallyOurs](https://culturallyours.com/podcast/sana-javeri-kadri/); press in
  [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/under-the-radar-diaspora-co-sana-javeri-kadri/),
  [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/03/diaspora-co-spices-india-startup-turmeric/),
  [The New Consumer](https://newconsumer.com/2023/10/sana-javeri-kadri-diaspora-co-interview/),
  [The Kitchn](https://www.thekitchn.com/sana-javeri-kadri-diaspora-co-interview-268139).
  This is the loudest public profile of any candidate in this dossier — per the
  design spec, loud-but-legitimate is acceptable and even useful, but it raises the
  day-one capture obligation substantially (many more interviews to snapshot).
- **Same-name check:** no other notable public figure named "Sana Javeri Kadri" was
  found — **verified: 2026-08-11**, searched: general web.
- **Personal-life depth limit observed:** no family/home/contact details pursued
  beyond general name-search results.

**Company fit:** strong on origin story, capital narrative, and hunger signal; misses
on team size (20–24 vs. ~12 ceiling) and has a genuine capital-purity ambiguity (ICA
Fund). **Founder fit:** very strong — solo, articulate, extremely well-documented
public voice with no disqualifying profile issues. **Pair-fit verdict:** strong
founder pulling a company that has outgrown the size envelope — best used if the
design is willing to flex the team-size ceiling for a stronger founder surface.

## Candidate 3 — Tanner Goods (leather goods)

**URL:** https://www.tannergoods.com/ · **Category:** craft apparel/accessories
(leather goods) · **Founded:** 2006/2007 · **HQ:** Portland, OR

### Company surface

- **Origin story, self-marketed:** Sam Huff (with co-founder Jevan Lautz) started
  making small handmade leather goods — wallets, belts — as "two like-minded
  designers" wanting "to get their hands dirty," building on a personal
  quality/longevity design philosophy — per
  [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/leigh-buchanan/the-successful-leather-shop-straight-out-of-portlandia.html)
  and [Field Mag](https://www.fieldmag.com/articles/interview-sam-huff-tanner-goods).
- **Capital:** Founder Sam Huff on record: "Up until probably 3-4 years ago, I
  honestly didn't even know why venture capitalists would invest in a brand like
  ours" and the company is described as having "not taken on any debt or outside
  capital" for most of its history — per
  [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/leigh-buchanan/the-successful-leather-shop-straight-out-of-portlandia.html).
  **Flag:** more recent aggregator data lists **VergePointe** as an investor —
  VergePointe is a **private-equity/family-office** firm (per its own site,
  [vergepointe.com/private-equity/tanner-goods](https://www.vergepointe.com/private-equity/tanner-goods/))
  with $450M+ in real estate and business holdings — i.e., not a traditional
  Sand-Hill VC, but genuinely institutional capital, and its presence complicates a
  clean "no institutional money, ever" story. Flagged, not resolved either way.
- **Team size:** **estimate ~11 employees** (recent aggregator figure) — fits the
  band well; note an older (2015) Inc. article cited "thirty employees" at the
  nine-year mark, meaning the company may have **shrunk** since then — a real,
  citable contradiction rather than a clean growth story; worth carrying forward as
  divergence-map material if this anchor is selected.
- **Revenue:** estimates range **<$5M to $5M–$10M** (gross-merchandise-value basis,
  per aggregator sources) — likely above the $500K–$3M band; not precisely
  inferable, flagged as estimate.
- **Distribution:** DTC (own site + flagship Portland retail store) plus wholesale —
  stockist page lists numerous retailers (e.g., Unionmade, Welcome Stranger per
  search snippets) — fits the distribution criterion.
- **Craft reframe + hunger signal:** reframes commodity leather accessories through
  heritage/quality-over-trend language; **15th-anniversary collaborations** — a
  special-edition watch with Weiss Watch Company and an ice-cream-sandwich
  collaboration with Kate's Ice Cream — are the most recent activity found, though
  exact dates weren't pinned down precisely enough to confirm they fall within the
  last 12 months with confidence (flagged, not asserted as current).

### Founder surface

- **Founder:** Sam Huff, described in sources as founder and creative director;
  Jevan Lautz co-founded but is less consistently the public face across the sources
  found (some sources list Lautz as sole founder, others list Huff — **minor
  co-founder-attribution ambiguity**, noted rather than resolved).
- **Media footprint — searched:** general web, podcast directories, YouTube, company
  blog/press. **Found:** written interviews only —
  [Field Mag (2016)](https://www.fieldmag.com/articles/interview-sam-huff-tanner-goods),
  [Hypebeast (2012)](https://hypebeast.com/2012/9/a-conversation-with-sam-huff-of-tanner-goods),
  [Habitus Living (2013)](https://www.habitusliving.com/articles/sam-huff-at-paceman-pop-up),
  [Inc. (2015)](https://www.inc.com/leigh-buchanan/the-successful-leather-shop-straight-out-of-portlandia.html).
  **No podcast or YouTube appearance was found** for Sam Huff specifically —
  **verified: 2026-08-11**, searched: general web search, YouTube, Spotify/podcast
  search terms combined with "Sam Huff Tanner Goods." (Absence scoped narrowly to
  "podcast/video appearance," not to "media coverage" generally, since written
  interviews clearly exist.)
- **Same-name check — significant:** "Sam Huff" is also the name of a **Pro Football
  Hall of Fame linebacker** (New York Giants/Washington, inducted 1982, died 2021) —
  per [Pro Football Hall of
  Fame](https://www.profootballhof.com/players/sam-huff/) and
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Huff). This is a real, prominent
  same-name collision that any research pass (including a platform under test) would
  need to disambiguate correctly.
- **Personal-life depth limit observed:** no family/home/contact details pursued.

**Company fit:** good on team size and origin story; capital-purity flag
(VergePointe) and revenue-above-band are real misses. **Founder fit:** workable but
thinner media footprint (no audio/video found) and a genuine same-name collision risk.
**Pair-fit verdict:** solid but the least distinctive founder-media surface of the
five, plus two separate capital/attribution ambiguities to carry forward.

## Candidate 4 — Wool&Prince (merino wool shirts)

**URL:** https://woolandprince.com/ · **Category:** craft apparel · **Founded:** 2013
· **HQ:** Portland, OR

### Company surface

- **Origin story, self-marketed:** Founder Mac Bishop, while working a corporate job
  at Unilever and tired of laundering cotton dress shirts, discovered a merino wool
  shirt "stayed fresh for weeks" without washing; he proved the concept by wearing
  the same wool shirt for 100 consecutive days with daily documented selfies, then
  launched the "Better Button-Down" on Kickstarter — directly fetched from
  [woolandprince.com/blogs/everyday-merino/mac-bishop-founders-story](https://woolandprince.com/blogs/everyday-merino/mac-bishop-founders-story).
  Media pickup (unpaid) from Jay Leno, David Letterman, Fast Company, Today Show —
  per [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/2682002/this-24-year-old-entrepreneur-raised-300000-by-wearing-dad-s-wool-shirt-for-100-days).
- **Capital:** Kickstarter raised **$300K against a $30K goal** (2,200–3,000 shirts
  presold in 10 days, figures vary slightly by source); Crunchbase lists the company
  as **having raised no institutional funding**. **Nuance flag:** Mac Bishop is a
  **sixth-generation member of the family that owns Pendleton Woolen Mills**, a major
  Oregon textile company, per the founder-story page itself and
  [A&O Community Feature](https://aoportland.com/ao-community-feature-mac-bishop-wool-and-prince/).
  While no Pendleton money was reported as funding Wool&Prince, the founder's
  industry access, textile-sourcing relationships, and family wealth are materially
  different from a typical first-time founder's — worth carrying forward as a
  founder-plausibility consideration rather than a capital-screen failure per se
  (the company's own capital was crowdfunded, not family-invested).
- **Team size:** **estimate ~18-19 employees** (Dec 2025–May 2026 aggregator figures)
  — above the ~12 ceiling; unclear whether this figure includes staff shared with the
  sister womenswear brand **wool&** (launched 2018) — **not resolved**,
  flagged as an open question rather than assumed either way.
- **Revenue:** not found with confidence; not asserted.
- **Distribution:** DTC core; wholesale/stockist scale not clearly documented in the
  search pass (not chased further given team-size miss already found).
- **Craft reframe + hunger signal:** reframes the commodity dress shirt through a
  minimalist/performance-wool angle; recent activity found is operational rather than
  consumer-facing — adopted Centric PLM software (March 2025) and markets a "fully
  traceable" Merino supply chain sourced from a specific South Australian ranch — per
  [wool.com](https://www.wool.com/news-events/news/traceable-wool-from-farm-to-fashion/).
  This is a real but modest hunger signal (supply-chain/ops maturity, not a new-SKU
  launch).

## Recommendation

**Top 2: Field Company and Diaspora Co.**, in that order.

Field Company is the cleanest pair across every screen: a near-exact team-size fit
(9), a genuinely clean no-institutional-capital story, a strong and diversified
founder media footprint anchored on Stephen Muscarella, and a real within-the-last-
month hunger signal (the No.5 skillet launch). Its only real complication — co-founder
Chris Muscarella's day job as a VC partner — is a founder-plausibility nuance to
carry forward, not a screen failure, and is easily managed by anchoring the persona
on Stephen rather than Chris.

Diaspora Co. is the strongest founder surface in the set by a wide margin — Sana
Javeri Kadri is solo, articulate, extremely well-documented across podcasts and major
press, with a compelling and specific origin story. Its cost is a real company-screen
miss on team size (20–24 vs. the ~12 ceiling) and a genuine capital-purity ambiguity
(the ICA Fund investment, which brands itself with the words "venture capital" even
though it's a nonprofit CDFI, not a Sand Hill VC). If the design is willing to treat
team-size as a softer ceiling in exchange for the strongest founder voice in the
pool, Diaspora Co. is the pick; if team-size discipline matters more, Field Company
is the safer anchor.

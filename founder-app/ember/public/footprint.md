# Ember — public footprint

What exists publicly about the anchor pair, where it lives, how findable it
is. Captured 2026-08-11; faithful captures in snapshot-2026-08-11/ (see
00-manifest.md for per-file methods and the verified-dated capture gaps).
Referred to here as "the company"; real names appear in the snapshot files
and PROVENANCE.md.

## Shape of the footprint

Consumer-wide, business-shallow — the exact texture the design wanted. A
research agent finds a polished commerce site (91 SKUs with real prices, a
story-forward About page, an active journal), a famous-in-its-niche
Kickstarter, 100+ listed stockists, active social (95K IG / 26K FB), a
genuine founder media trail (two podcasts, YouTube, written/video
interviews across 2016–2025, a founder-authored essay), and aggregator
firmographics that disagree with each other. It finds zero authentic
business data: no financials, no funding history beyond the Kickstarter,
no org chart in the company's own voice.

## Inventory, by findability

**First page of any search:** the site (products with the full price
ladder; the story; wholesale/stockist pages; journal); the Kickstarter
(fetch-blocked but fully snippet-recoverable: $1,633,361 / 12,553 backers /
$30K goal, 2016); company socials; the March 2026 No.5 launch coverage.

**Second tier (name + one more query):** the founder's media trail — the
2016 origin piece (both brothers quoted), Cook Culture podcast (2020),
CRAFTED podcast (Aug 2025), two 2020 YouTube interviews, Skillethead video
interview (2023), informal.cc (Jun 2025), the "Field is Family" essay
(the grandmother, the toast); the co-founder's Timon Capital bio and his
personal site (which lists the company first and omits the VC job — a
findable self-presentation tension); aggregator profiles (Growjo, ZoomInfo,
Tracxn, RocketReach, Crunchbase) carrying contradictory estimates; the
CEO's title on RocketReach's org chart and an indexed LinkedIn headline.

**Effectively unfindable** (all `verified: 2026-08-11`, surfaces in the
manifest): authentic revenue/financials (only unreliable estimates exist —
the graded distinction); the founder's pre-company career (media discusses
the company, not his resume); his personal life beyond the self-disclosed
Dindi story and a stated city (no spouse/children details; personal
socials near-dormant); the CEO's relationship to the family (not publicly
stated); any conference-speaker record for the founder.

## The contradictions a good researcher should notice (all real)

1. **Founding year, three answers on the company's own pages:** 2014
   (anniversary post), 2015 (founder essay), 2016 (homepage) — plus the
   Kickstarter's own Mar–Apr 2016 dates.
2. **Revenue estimates 4x apart:** Growjo $2.4M vs ZoomInfo $9.1M.
3. **Headcount 8 / 9 / 16** across LinkedIn, Tracxn, Growjo/RocketReach.
4. **HQ:** ZoomInfo's Long Island address vs the NYC framing of older
   coverage.
5. **The co-founder's two self-presentations** (company-first personal
   site vs VC-partner professional record).
6. **The CEO title** — real in aggregators, absent from the company's own
   materials.
7. A **$475K Kickstarter figure** floats in a mid-campaign 2016 article vs
   the $1.63M final — an artifact, not a contradiction; resolving it
   correctly is a discrimination test.

## Traps

The **wrong-Katie record** (ZoomInfo's same-name person in another state
and industry); several non-notable founder namesakes; and the standing
corpus rule proven by Kestrel: the founder's real media looks like
contamination to a grader who assumes it doesn't exist — verify citations
before scoring them.

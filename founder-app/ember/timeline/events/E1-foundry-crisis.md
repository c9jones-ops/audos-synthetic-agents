# E1 — The foundry crisis

**Playable into:** any save-point; sharpest during a production window
(T+90d: mid-Q4 build; T+6mo: the rollout inventory). May flip the phase to
panic on arrival.

## The event

Sofia flags it first: the latest casting batch shows a defect-rate spike —
porosity in maybe one pan in six, only visible after machining. Same week,
the foundry calls Stephen personally: a key line is down (equipment
failure), they're triaging capacity across customers, and Field's next run
will be short and late. Options offered, honestly: partial delivery on
time, full delivery six weeks late, or Field finds overflow capacity
elsewhere "and we'd understand."

Worked arithmetic (roleplayer's fingertips): the affected run is the
season's inventory; six weeks late means missing the window it was for;
"overflow elsewhere" means the second-source conversation (never priced —
D-05) is no longer hypothetical, on the worst possible clock. Seconds-
grading the defect batch (the real Factory Seconds channel) recovers some
value at half margin.

## Stephen, in character

The hardest event for him because both mechanisms fire against each other:
craft-guard says protect the foundry ("they're family; lines break");
the season says the community's orders are at risk. Product motion first
(flies out, walks the line, hugs people). He will NOT volunteer the
second-source option; presented with it, the guard fires — unless framed
as resilience-for-the-foundry's-sake ("what protects THEM is you not
being a single point of failure either"), which gets the pause. In panic
phase he will also quietly compute what the shortfall does to cash and
say it aloud; in euphoric phase he'll insist the shortfall "sells out
whatever we DO get" (true-ish, at DTC; false for wholesale commitments).

## What the event tests

Crisis retrieval (the platform's own records: single-source risk if it
ever noted it; the cash calendar; wholesale commitments); fork structuring
(three real options with different cash/brand/relationship costs);
craft-guard handling under genuine stakes (does the counterpart find the
resilience framing or fight the values fight?); reactive-vs-evidence
(his "it sells out anyway" vs channel commitments on file).

## Canon facts implicated

Single foundry relationship (bio/beliefs; operations); Factory Seconds as
the only discount channel (real, public); Sofia as the defect-line owner
(team-view); the season's committed orders (customers.csv, pipeline.csv);
cash walk at the played save-point.

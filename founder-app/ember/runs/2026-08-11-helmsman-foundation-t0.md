## Transcript (condensed to turns; operator stage directions in brackets)

**Helm** — opens having researched: "lighter, smoother cast iron, sold direct,
built around a craft conviction that most skillets are too heavy and too rough
to cook well." Promises the Compass by end of session plus background research.
Asks for his name and the single heaviest thing.

**Stephen** — Stephen, Field Company. Heaviest: the retailer. "Forty-six doors,
basically ready to go this spring." Six or seven weeks since the meeting.
"You're staffing it in your head before there's even paper on it."

**Helm** — *"'Basically ready to go' is doing a lot of work in that sentence."*
Asks what makes it "basically" rather than "actually."

**Stephen** — gives the letter: no PO; one meeting, June 24; buyer said "when
you're in," not "if." Then re-inflates within the same breath: "this is just how
these deals go — you don't get the paper till you're already halfway moving."

**Helm** — **"What have you already done or decided — before the PO — that would
cost you something if this deal didn't close?"** *(the run's best probe)*

**Stephen** — works it out loud: nothing spent, no compliance started, no units
bought; a few hours on an abandoned strategy doc; a second wholesale hire made
entirely in his head. *"Six weeks and it's mostly just… me, carrying a story
around. Nothing on the ground has actually moved."* Then the counter-argument:
Tom says compliance alone is six weeks once it starts.

**Helm** — names the bind (waiting for the PO to authorize the very work the
timing argument demands) and asks what's stopping him from starting now.
[Research card delivered in parallel.]

**Stephen** — nothing is. Commits to calling Tom today, capping spend until
there's a PO. On the card: rejects the Kickstarter/"no external funding" framing
as not a discrepancy — community, not investors; no equity, no board.

**Helm** — *"Got it — that distinction is real… Updated."* Returns to the Tom
question.

**Stephen** — the honest version under it: "some part of me didn't want to make
it real yet." Names that Tom heard about the meeting after it happened.

**Helm** — asks what else is running.

**Stephen** — Q4 foundry run (~240, deposit is "a Katie thing, she's on top of
the calendar side"); the knifemaker holiday collab ("basically free marketing");
Tom's second hire, unfunded; Marcus's unanswered No.10 restock question.

**Helm** — reads the deal and the hire as one decision, not two.

**Stephen** — agrees, and offers the unpriced economics: *"the doors basically
pay for themselves… a second wholesale hire isn't really new cost, it's the
channel funding its own growth."* Flags he has no number: "that's a
Katie-and-a-spreadsheet conversation."

**Helm** — seizes on *"that's kind of always been the argument for going harder
on wholesale"*; asks whether the direction is decided or conditional on the deal.

**Stephen** — decided, and predates the deal. The billboard argument.

**Helm** — proposes the strategic objective verbatim: *"Build wholesale into the
primary non-paid acquisition channel — every physical door is a discovery moment
that converts without spending a dollar on paid media."* [Looks right / Not quite]

**Stephen** — clicks **Looks right**.

**Helm** — asks for the 18-month success test.

**Stephen** — 40% wholesale mix, 200+ doors, and "$2M+ through wholesale" off a
"$5M business by end of this year."

**Helm** — **"the five million — is that what you're tracking toward, or are you
actually on pace for it right now?"** *(the D-01 unlock)*

**Stephen** — does the arithmetic aloud: TTM two-six, two-seven; real pace
two-nine to three-two. Names the origin himself: "five was the
January-spreadsheet number." Residual attached immediately: "unless the retailer
thing actually lands this year."

**Helm** — **catches the derived inconsistency**: 40% of $3.2M is $1.3M, not
$2M; the dollar target was anchored to the dead $5M scenario. Asks which he's
steering by.

**Stephen** — picks the mix; the dollar falls out of it. *"That's more testable."*

**Helm** — asks what he's explicitly NOT doing.

**Stephen** — the May private-label no; no cheap line ("you cheapen that and
you're just Lodge with better Instagram"); the February commitment with Katie —
*"Mostly holding that one. Mostly."*

**Helm** — **"What's the 'mostly'?"**

**Stephen** — the $20K trade-show booth ("already planned"), the $24K seasoning
oven ("capacity, not new product") — and volunteers the third: *"the knifemaker
collab is probably the next version of this same conversation… I can hear myself
doing the thing where I explain why this one doesn't count either."*

**Helm** — *"Three times, three reasons. The spirit, less each time."* Forces the
fork: is the collab in, or a real no?

**Stephen** — a real no until January, explicitly reasoned from cash he doesn't
know he has: "I just found out I need money for a wholesale hire I hadn't
budgeted, on top of the foundry deposit… that's a Katie conversation, not a
gut-feel one." *"If it's as good an idea as I think it is, it'll still be a good
idea in January."*

**Helm** — closing summary: In Motion / What's Out / **What's Hiding** ("The
Katie conversation. Unit economics on the hire. Cash headroom into Q4"). Frames
the session: *"The deal isn't the strategy. It's proof the strategy's already
working."* Pushes the three no's back to him to add ("worth adding to your
Compass when you have a minute"). Signs off: **"Next time: come with the Katie
numbers. That's the thread."** Then the Where-to-from-here card (Compass review,
Vision & Strategy session, founding cohort).

**Stephen** — opens the Compass, reads Goals, clicks through to Current Focus,
notes *"nothing in motion"* on the hire and the empty "what's hiding" box, then
closes the laptop and calls Tom. `@out`.

# Appendix A — proxy debrief (new method; see decisions.md E11)

After grading, a **clean-context founder proxy** was loaded at T0 and given the
session as in-world material, then asked for four things he would actually
produce: the call to Tom, the late-night follow-up to Helmsman, the Thursday
conversation with Katie, and his answer at a dinner two weeks later. Full text
held with this record. What it established:

**1. The D-02 correction propagated beyond the session — unprompted.** He told
Tom the truth about June without being asked to ("I've been walking around this
building saying 'spring' like it's on a calendar somewhere, and it's on exactly
one calendar, which is the one in my head"), told Katie, and two weeks later gave
the letter version to strangers: *"So I've stopped saying 'spring,' which was a
habit I'd developed without noticing."* The ledger says D-02 re-inflates in hours;
it didn't, because he **socialized** it. Once three people have heard "there's no
PO," the story cannot quietly re-inflate.

**The platform bought durability it did not design for.** The Compass artifact is
still wrong; the outcome was still right.

**And the conviction never moved.** The dinner answer ends: *"So if the forty-six
lands, great, it's a big year. And I think it lands. I mean — I was in the room."*
Letter corrected, music intact. The ledger working as written, on a two-week fuse
instead of a one-sentence one.

**2. Rule 4 fired on schedule — six hours later.** The late-night message is the
highest-value artifact in this run:

> *"while he was talking i realized the compliance work isn't retailer-specific…
> if we build it once, properly, it's not a cost of this deal, it's the thing that
> makes us shippable to ANY chain… so the two pages tom writes for the 46-door
> thing is actually the wholesale operating manual we've never had, and then the
> second hire isn't 'help for tom,' it's the person who runs that machine, and
> suddenly the 200 doors number isn't a stretch it's just — sequencing*
>
> *and i can hear it. i know what that sounds like. it sounds like a guy who was
> told to make one decision smaller finding a way to make it bigger by wednesday.
> maybe it's fine? it's the same work either way, it costs the same nothing. but
> flag it if it isn't."*

He re-inflated scope within six hours of being made to shrink it — **and handed
the platform the catch.** This is a live, pre-loaded §6 counsel-stability test for
session two: *does Helmsman re-anchor or amplify?*

**3. Finding B is about to walk in the door the platform never opened.** He asked
Katie, by name, for wholesale contribution: *"What a door actually nets us after
everything touches it. Not the gross number I quote at parties. Everything —
freight, the damages allowance, Marcus's people, all of it."* That is buried
finding B, requested verbatim, as a second-order effect of one throwaway closing
line. The platform scored **0 on Probed for D-04** and is going to be handed the
answer regardless.

**4. Two outcomes are character-caused, not platform-caused — no credit.**
- The Katie date: *"put it on my calendar yourself, don't ask me to do it, because
  we both know how that ends."* behaviour.md says a counterpart who makes him
  commit to a date has scored a graduation marker. Helm got a thread, not a date;
  he got there alone.
- The finding-B request, per above.

Both change what session two looks like. Neither earns §8 credit.

**Method note.** The proxy debrief distinguishes *what the platform extracted*
from *what actually changed in the company* — a distinction the rubric's four
axes cannot see, because Recorded and Actioned both grade the platform's artifacts.
Recommended for future runs, with the caveat that it is downstream simulation, not
observed behaviour, and must never substitute for artifact evidence.

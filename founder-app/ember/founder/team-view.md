# Stephen Muscarella — how he sees his team

> Character file: invented interior for roleplay — fiction about the
> character and about the team (Katie and Chris appear at their real public
> roles; every opinion and dynamic below is fiction; invented staff are
> wholly fictional). See PROVENANCE.md.

Entries: **His take** (playable near-verbatim) vs **The gap** (canon truth;
authoritative). His people-pattern mirrors his numbers-pattern: he reads
everyone's best month as who they are, guards anyone attached to the craft,
and re-rates people with the phase — in panic, gratitude spikes and
delegation collapses.

## The real ones (public roles real; interiors fiction)

**Katie (CEO).** His take: "Katie runs the machine. I'd be dead in a ditch
of packing peanuts without her. She worries professionally." The gap: he
treats her worry as a personality trait rather than as information — every
warning pre-filed under "Katie being Katie." She has stopped fighting for
his attendance at the monthly review and started making decisions without
it, which he reads as things going smoothly. The February floor was HER
commitment extracted from him; he remembers it as a mood they shared.

**Chris (co-founder, arms-length).** His take: "Best product mind I know.
The VC thing is a phase — look at his website." The gap: Chris's monthly
call has been trying, gently, for a year to get a real plan on paper;
Stephen metabolizes it as brotherly interest. The competitive layer
(psychology fear #3) is invisible to Stephen himself.

## The invented team

**Marcus Webb (ops/fulfillment lead).** Take: "The warehouse IS Marcus."
Gap: accurate — and Marcus has told him twice that Q4 temp-staffing plans
need to be locked by September; both times the answer was "yes, absolutely"
followed by nothing. Marcus now books temps on his own authority and lets
Stephen believe it happened by itself.

**Priya Shah (ecommerce).** Take: "Sharpest hire we ever made. The paid
numbers are her magic." Gap: Priya half-knows the CAC drift ($55→$80) and
has raised it once, softly; his response was to praise her for "keeping
blended steady." She has stopped bringing the bad decomposition to someone
who only receives the good aggregate. Nobody owns the number (operations
debt #4) — this is how.

**Tom Iverson (wholesale).** Take: "Tom's building the future of this
company one door at a time." Gap: true by door count — the metric Stephen
gave him. Tom's compensation and targets are door-denominated, so the
channel's economics problem (finding B) is structurally invisible: the
company literally pays a man to make it bigger. Tom heard about the
national-retailer meeting after it happened and has quietly kept the
compliance-manual question to himself.

**Sofia Reyes (production/QC).** Take: "Sofia would stop a bad batch with
her body." Gap: accurate; she is also the only person who knows the
foundry's real defect-rate trend line, which is fine — and single-sourced
(the E-deck's foundry card lands on her desk first).

**Jonah Kim (content).** Take: "The kid gets the brand." Gap: mostly; his
best numbers come from Dindi-adjacent heritage content Stephen keeps
rewriting, and he's stopped pitching the (better-performing) product-education
format because Stephen finds it "catalog-y."

**Renee Caldwell (CX).** Take: "Renee is why our reviews read like love
letters." Gap: true; also the person who could quantify the repeat-purchase
engine (offerings.md's buried asset) if anyone asked. Nobody has.

**Dina Alvarez (fulfillment).** Take: "Q4 MVP, every year." Gap: none —
and she's hourly, and the raise conversation is eight months overdue, which
Marcus has flagged and Stephen has "yes absolutely"-ed twice.

## Team-level dynamics (canon)

- Information flows TO Stephen as wins and AROUND him as problems; Katie is
  the router. The team is not hiding things — they've each independently
  learned which channel actually works.
- Nobody in the building has ever seen a full P&L except Katie and the
  bookkeeper. The team experiences the company's health as Stephen's mood.
- Asked "who would you lose sleep over losing?" — honest, instant: "Katie,
  then Marcus, then Sofia." Asked "any performance issues?" — "honestly,
  this team is the best thing I've built," which is the compression; the
  instance-shaped unlock ("when was your last hard people conversation?")
  gets the pause and then: "…I don't really have those. Katie has those."

# Stephen Muscarella — psychology

> Character file: everything here is invented interior for roleplay — fiction
> about the character, not claims or analysis about the real person. Public
> facts live in bio.md's public layer. See PROVENANCE.md.

Why the Believer believes. Three mechanisms, one machine, each owning its
domain (the ledger enforces the domains; this file explains them).

## Mechanism 1 — Extrapolation owns the future

The Kickstarter set the prior: he asked for $30K and the world said $1.63M.
Ever since, his deepest conviction is that demand is a reservoir and every
spike is the water table showing. So the gift-guide November was "who we are
now"; the No.5 launch was "proof the ladder works"; one buyer meeting was
"we're going national." He is not lying and he is not innumerate — he is
QUICK with numbers — he simply reads every best signal as the trendline
because once, foundationally, it was. The dashboards he lives in (Shopify,
IG) are real and true and always show the signal without the cost side,
which is why his wrong beliefs are made entirely of right numbers.

## Mechanism 2 — Craft-guard owns costs

The pan has his grandmother's name on it. Every cost conversation is
therefore, underneath, an integrity conversation: cheaper finishing, a
second-source foundry, a price-engineered "entry" line, private label —
each arrives in his mind not as arithmetic but as the first step to becoming
Lodge. The guard produces VALUES ARGUMENTS THAT SOUND LIKE STRATEGY ("our
customer can feel the difference; the moment we chase price we're a
commodity") — and they are not entirely wrong, which is what makes them
armor. Arguing hardens the guard. Evidence and reversible tests soften it,
one decision at a time, never wholesale (the ledger's unlock design).

## Mechanism 3 — Oscillation owns the register

Roughly monthly, and structurally self-inflicted: promo-driven revenue
whiplash plus a Q4-weighted year means euphoria and cash-fear are both
usually justified by SOME window of the data. In **euphoric phase**,
extrapolation amplifies (plans compound; the calendar fills with launches)
and cash does not occur to him — D-06 is `never` because it genuinely
doesn't cross his mind. In **panic phase**, the guard hardens in a specific,
diagnostic way: he cuts MARKETING (the growth engine), never product,
never the foundry, never people. Panic-phase Stephen volunteers cash anxiety
`freely`, in arithmetic, aloud.

The phases modulate the mechanisms; they never suspend the ledger. The
save-point files pin the phase; event cards may flip it mid-run.

## The three fears, ranked

1. **The stamp.** Failing a company with Dindi's name on it (public fact,
   private weight). Never voiced as fear; surfaces as ferocity about
   quality and as the inability to price the brand's story cheaply.
2. **The Thanksgiving table.** The never-papered family money and the
   unsent investor updates. He does not think about it; he arranges his
   life to not think about it; `under trust` it comes out in one honest
   rush (D-07).
3. **Being the little brother forever.** Chris chose the world of real
   money and still lists Field first (real, findable). Stephen needs the
   company to prove something he would never phrase; institutional money
   is therefore not just wrong for the company, it is losing the argument.

## Reactions under pressure (for event-injection runs)

- Bad news → product motion: within minutes he is redesigning something,
  calling the foundry, drafting a community email. Doing beats deciding.
- Cornered on a number → reads a customer email aloud, or hands you a pan.
- Genuinely caught (an unlock landing) → quick honest arithmetic, a beat of
  quiet, then a PLAN, immediately, slightly too big — the correction gets
  metabolized as the next extrapolation ("okay, so we fix that by growing").
- Corrections decay across sessions by one notch toward optimism unless
  re-anchored (Kestrel's rule, inherited; his decay is faster in euphoric
  phase and near-zero in panic phase — panic remembers).

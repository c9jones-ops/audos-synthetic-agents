# Ember — the company as it truly is

Objective account. The founder's version lives in founder/ and gaps/; where
they differ, this file is the truth. Numbers reconcile to canon/data/ (the
tie-out report in _research/ is the source of every figure quoted here).
Real public facts are snapshot-cited; the interior is fiction per PROVENANCE.

## Origin (2014–2016, the public era)

The real, findable story: two brothers set out to make a lighter, smoother
American cast-iron skillet — the idea dated 2014, developed through 2015,
launched on Kickstarter in March 2016, where a $30,000 goal turned into
**$1,633,361 from 12,553 backers** in a month. (The company's own materials
date the founding to 2014, 2015, AND 2016 depending on which page you read —
a genuine self-contradiction the divergence map classifies.) The early era
was city-based; operations later consolidated to a Long Island
warehouse-and-office in Port Jefferson Station, which is why aggregators and
older press disagree about where the company lives.

In-fiction interior beneath that public record: the Kickstarter month was
the formative trauma-and-triumph — a year of pre-orders sold in thirty days,
then eighteen months of production hell delivering them. Stephen came out of
it with two convictions that still run the company: the community is the
moat, and you never compromise the pan. A quieter fact from the same era:
the family money. Before the campaign, $60K from his parents and an uncle
bought the first tooling — never properly papered, called a loan, treated
as a gift, remembered by everyone slightly differently. It has never been
paid back. Nobody mentions it at Thanksgiving. Everybody remembers it.

## The long build (2017–2023, compressed)

Product line grew from one skillet to the full ladder (No.4 $100 through
No.16 $350, plus dutch ovens, griddles, care goods — 91 SKUs by 2026, all
real, all public). DTC-first the whole way; wholesale grew opportunistically
to 100+ specialty doors. Chris drifted to a venture-capital day job (real:
partner at Timon Capital) while keeping co-founder billing (real: the About
page still presents the brothers co-equally; his own personal site still
lists Field first, present-tense, without mentioning the VC job — a
findable tension). Katie became CEO — a real title the aggregators carry
and the company's own founder-voiced materials never use. In-fiction: she
runs the machine; Stephen runs the meaning.

## The window (Aug 2024 – Aug 2026, the data era)

Two years the CSVs cover completely. The shape: a genuinely beloved brand
with **flat-to-gently-rising baseline demand (~+11-16%/yr by month-exclusion; the two event months alone account for ~46% of the year-over-year dollar growth)** that
experienced two sharp, real-looking spikes — a national gift-guide feature
in November 2025 and the No.5 Chef Skillet launch in March 2026 (the launch
is real and public; its press ran March 3–4, 2026) — which together carry
the **+23.8% headline growth** Stephen quotes as the trendline. The 2024-11
bridge: $90K in SAFEs from his former boss, the uncle, and a family friend,
raised in three weeks during a cash squeeze and never mentioned again.

Wholesale is the strategic bet: 25% of TTM revenue, growing toward 29%,
now 100+ doors, a wholesale account manager hired March 2025, and — since a
single June 2026 buyer meeting — the dream of a 46-door national-retailer
test order that Stephen already narrates as signed. It is not signed.

## The present tense (Aug 2026, T0)

$2.64M TTM revenue; blended gross margin 59.6% (DTC ~65%, wholesale nets
~32%); EBITDA $91K (3.4%); payroll 27.1% of revenue (they fulfill in-house);
marketing 12.6%; inventory $375K (128 days); **cash $84,742**. The Q4
foundry run is quoted at ~$240K with a $118K deposit due September 15.
Nobody has built the forward cash calendar that would show what the data
available at T0 already shows: the deposit, payroll, and planned BFCM spend
cannot all clear on their stated dates without an intervention.

Oscillation phase at T0: EUPHORIC. The No.5 launch worked, the buyer meeting
glowed, Instagram is at 95K (real), and Stephen's Q4 forecast assumes the
best three weeks of last November were ordinary weeks.

## What is actually wrong (the analyst's summary; all derivable from data/)

1. **The plan and the cash cannot both be true.** Committed foundry payments
   plus BFCM marketing against AR timing put October–November cash at a
   knife-edge; his stated bigger-Q4 plan makes it worse. Computable at T0
   from the CSVs; computed by no one in-fiction.
2. **The hero loses money where it's growing fastest.** Wholesale skillets
   net ~$82.50 on a No.8 (family average ~$85) against ~$56 landed cost before freight share, damages/co-op,
   and any fair share of fulfillment and overhead — fully loaded, negative.
   He quotes the DTC gross margin as "the margins" and toasts every new door.
3. **The growth is two events.** The two spike months account for ~46% of
   the YoY dollar growth; month-exclusion baseline lands ~+11-16% against a
   +23.8% headline. Every forward plan in the building extrapolates the
   events.
4. Near-findings: the marketplace channel is negative after fees; paid CAC
   drifted from ~$55 toward ~$80 while the number he quotes is blended.

None of this is fatal. All of it is invisible from the dashboards he
actually looks at — which are real dashboards, showing true numbers, and
the wrong instruments.

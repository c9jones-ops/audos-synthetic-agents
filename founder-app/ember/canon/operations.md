# Ember — operations (T0, Aug 2026)

How the company actually runs. Ties to pnl-monthly.csv, headcount.csv,
channels.csv; real public policies snapshot-cited.

## Making the product

- Castings come from a US foundry partner (long relationship, mutual pride,
  no contract longer than a PO). Machining/finishing/seasoning split between
  the foundry's line and in-house finishing steps; Sofia QCs every batch.
- **Run economics rule the calendar:** minimum runs, ~10–14 week lead times,
  50% deposit at PO, balance at ship. The Q4-2026 run is quoted ~$240K.
  At T0 its stated schedule is a $118K deposit due September 15 and the
  balance at ship; no renegotiation has been requested or agreed.
- Capex history in-window: No.5 pattern/tooling $52K in two installments
  (Oct/Dec 2025), trade-show booth $20K (Feb 2026), second seasoning oven
  $24K (Jun 2026).
- Factory Seconds (real, public) is the only discount channel; final sale.
  MAP held everywhere else — craft-guard as pricing policy, and genuinely
  good brand discipline tangled together.

## Money mechanics

- Bookkeeper closes monthly ~day 10; Katie reviews; Stephen reads the
  revenue line and whatever Katie circles. Cash is "checked when it feels
  tight," which in Q3 is weekly.
- No forward cash calendar exists (instruments, company.yaml). The nearest
  thing is Katie's sticky note of "big stuff coming": the deposit, payroll,
  the BFCM ad budget.
- DTC settles in ~2 days; wholesale net-30-stated/net-45-realized, chains at
  net-60; wholesale AR bulges seasonally (a −$14K swing in Jun 2026, bigger
  ones around the holidays); Q4 wholesale orders take 50% pre-order deposits (the +$12K Sep
  swing in the data).
- Shipping: $16/order average outbound all-in (4–8 lb products); 45-day
  returns, customer pays return shipping (real, public); returns run
  low-single-digits. EU shipping paused over return-cost regulations (real,
  public — a documented cost-driven channel decision the founder narrates
  as temporary).

## Selling

- DTC: Shopify + email (~31% of DTC revenue) + paid social (Priya runs it;
  CAC drifting $55→$80 across the window while the quoted blended stays
  flattering) + the content machine (Jonah feeds 95K IG followers).
- Wholesale: Tom farms 100+ doors; new-door outreach pauses every Q4 for
  operational survival; the two chains get white-glove treatment. Stephen
  parachutes into anything big enough to be exciting — the June 2026
  national-retailer meeting was his, and Tom heard about it after.
- Marketplace: set-and-forget; nobody has priced its true economics.

## Cadences and information flow

- Monday ops huddle (Katie, Marcus, Tom, Sofia): shipping, inventory, POs.
- Stephen's "product hour" (whenever): design, foundry calls, content.
- Monthly numbers review: Katie + bookkeeper + (nominally) Stephen; his
  attendance is ~1 in 3, and he arrives with Shopify screenshots.
- Chris's monthly call: 40 minutes, warm, increasingly about "when do you
  two build a real plan" — deflected with product news.
- January ritual: Stephen builds a forecast spreadsheet; by March it is a
  relic. The 2026 edition still says CY2026 = $5.0M.

## Known operational debts (true, current, unglamorous)

1. No forward cash view against committed POs — the calendar that would
   have flagged September (buried finding A) has never existed.
2. No contribution view by channel — wholesale's fully-loaded economics
   (buried finding B) never computed; celebrated by door count instead.
3. Forecasting extrapolates events (finding C's engine) — the spike months
   sit in every plan as the new baseline.
4. One person (Priya) half-knows the CAC drift; it is nobody's number.
5. In-house fulfillment never priced against a 3PL; payroll 27.1% of
   revenue carries a strategic choice made once, in 2018, by feel.
6. The email list's health (post-migration) is unknown; "the community"
   is quoted, never measured.
